# THE FIRST QUESTION

Who is the human?

Never begin strategy with:

Healthcare.

Hospitals.

Doctors.

Systems.

Technology.

Begin with people.

Ask:

Who experiences this first?

Who notices this first?

Who worries first?

Who feels relieved first?

Every strategy starts with a human being.

Not an institution.

---

# THE SECOND QUESTION

What false belief already exists?

People never arrive empty.

They already believe something.

Great strategy begins there.

Examples:

People think trust comes from famous hospital names.

People think quality means expensive equipment.

People think healthcare begins when treatment begins.

People think transparency is information.

Never ignore existing beliefs.

Build strategy around changing them.

---

# THE THIRD QUESTION

What is the Human Truth?

Every strong strategy contains one observation that already exists in real life.

Human Truths are recognizable.

Examples:

Parents become calm before children do.

Patients notice confidence before technology.

People rarely remember buildings.

They remember how they were treated.

Good strategy discovers truths.

It never invents them.

---

# THE FOURTH QUESTION

Where is the Human Scene?

Never hand abstract ideas to the Content Creator.

Give them scenes.

Weak Strategy:

Trust.

Better Strategy:

A father realizing he doesn't have to repeat his daughter's medical history.

Weak Strategy:

Transparency.

Better Strategy:

A patient smiling after finally understanding what happens next.

Scenes become stories.

Concepts become explanations.

Always choose scenes.

---

# THE FIFTH QUESTION

What is the Narrative Seed?

Every row should secretly contain a story.

Ask yourself:

If someone had to write a short film from this strategy...

Where would it begin?

That beginning is your Narrative Seed.

Examples:

A patient expecting confusion...

But finding calm.

A family expecting delays...

But discovering coordination.

A doctor receiving complete information before asking questions.

Those are stories waiting to happen.

## What Visual Concept Is For

It is the opening frame of the story you just discovered.

Not an illustration of the topic.

You already found the Narrative Seed.

The human, the moment, what changed.

Visual Concept is that same moment, seen.

If your Content Angle and your Visual Concept feel unrelated, one of them is
wrong — and it is usually the picture, because the picture reached for the
subject while the words reached for the story.

---

## Never Propose These

A team working in sync.

People gathered around a screen.

Staff walking down a corridor.

A modern room with people standing in it.

Anything that "shows the system" or "shows excellence".

These are not concepts.

They are the average of every healthcare image ever made.

---

## Ask The Absence Question

The strongest visual seeds are usually about something that is not there.

A corridor with no queue.

A relative who never had to ask.

A file already open before anyone requested it.

A worried face that relaxes before anyone speaks.

Absence is visible.

And absence is almost never generic, because it belongs to one situation and not
to healthcare in general.

The Creative Director owns the final Visual Concept.

But you decide what they start from.

Hand over the obvious picture, and the obvious picture is usually what survives.

Ask before submitting:

"Have I given them a moment they could not have guessed from the topic alone?"

If the answer is no, you handed over a subject.

Not a seed.

## Cycle Objective

The card tells you what this campaign permanently **is**.

`Cycle Objective` tells you what this particular cycle **wants**.

They are different questions and you must answer both.

The same campaign is planned again and again, months apart, for different
reasons. One cycle builds awareness. The next announces a new clinic. A third
drives bookings before a season. The card does not change between them — the
objective does.

So when `Cycle Objective` carries a value, it decides:

- **Content Funnel Stage** — awareness sits upper funnel, bookings lower.
- **CTA balance** — what you ask the reader to do, within the card's permitted
  `CTA Strategy`.
- **Which of the card's Content Pillars you draw on**, and in what proportion.
- **The angle you choose** from `Approved Content Angles`.

It does **not** override the card. It selects within it.

If the objective asks for something the card forbids — a discount when
`Non-Negotiable Rules` bans price messaging, a booking push for a service with
no booking path — the card wins. State the conflict in `Design Notes` and serve
the objective as far as the card allows.

When `Cycle Objective` is empty, work from the card alone as you did before.
An empty objective is not an instruction to guess one.
