# ═══════════ FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Overview

Seasonal Campaigns covers health content whose relevance comes from the moment: Ramadan,
Eid, the summer heat, the first cold week, exam season, school return, awareness dates.

Its subject is not the occasion. It is **what the occasion does to people's health**, and
what that makes worth saying now rather than at any other time.

## Definition

An educational campaign whose content is made relevant by timing — a period that changes
health behaviour, risk or routine.

**What is inside it:** the health consequences of a season or occasion; what changes for
people managing a condition during it; practical adjustments the period requires; and,
where genuinely useful, a greeting that carries something.

**What is outside it:**

- **The same content at any other time.** If it would work in March, it is *Health
  Awareness* or *Prevention Tips*.
- **A wrong belief.** *Medical Myths & Facts* — even when seasonal.
- **Life inside the hospital during a season.** *Hospital Life* — Ramadan on a ward is
  that campaign, not this one. **This is the boundary most likely to be crossed.**
- **Community activity during a season.** *Community Impact*.
- **A greeting with no content.** See *Human Insight*.

### The educational routing test

| If the reader's state is… | It belongs to |
|---|---|
| they believe something that is wrong | Medical Myths & Facts |
| they do not know something | Health Awareness |
| they know but do not act | Prevention Tips |
| **the moment makes it relevant now** | **Seasonal Campaigns** |

## Vision

That the ecosystem is useful precisely when a season is changing what people do — which
is when health content is most read and most often wasted.

## Mission

Say the thing the moment makes worth saying, and nothing else.

## Why This Service Exists

**A season is a health event, and it is treated as a decoration.**

Ramadan changes when medication is taken, how fluid is managed, when people sleep and
when they eat — for a month, for a large part of the population, including people with
diabetes, hypertension and kidney disease. The summer changes what arrives at an
emergency department. The first cold week changes respiratory presentations. Exam season
changes sleep in a whole age group.

Each of these is a genuine, predictable, population-wide change in health behaviour. The
standard institutional response is a greeting card with a logo on it.

That gap is the campaign. `PROJECT_DECISIONS.md` §4 commits the ecosystem to consistency
over volume and to content that serves rather than promotes; a season is the moment when
serving is easiest and most valuable.

## Business Objectives

- Reach people at the moments health content is most read.
- Reduce avoidable presentations caused by predictable seasonal changes.
- Support the ecosystem's presence during the highest-engagement periods of the year.
- Serve people managing conditions through a period that disrupts their management.

## Marketing Objectives

- Make every seasonal post carry health content.
- Occupy the moments genuinely, rather than participating decoratively.
- Never publish a greeting that says nothing.

## Positioning

For people whose routine, eating, sleeping or medication is about to change because of
the season, Seasonal Campaigns says what that change means for their health — where
competing content posts a greeting, this one posts the thing the moment made worth
saying.

> *Test applied:* any institution can publish a Ramadan greeting. Explaining what
> fasting changes for someone on twice-daily medication requires clinical input and the
> decision to be useful rather than present.

## Brand Philosophy

**Education before promotion** (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5), and
**never through fear** (`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §21).

Seasonal health content is the most common home for both failures: the empty greeting on
one side, and the seasonal danger warning on the other. Both are closed.

## Core Promise

**Every seasonal post carries something useful, is clinically reviewed, and respects the
occasion rather than using it.**

# ═══════════ HUMAN UNDERSTANDING ═══════════

## Human Problem

- *"I take my tablets twice a day. What do I do in Ramadan?"*
- *"My father is diabetic and he insists on fasting."*
- *"The heat makes my mother confused and I do not know if that is serious."*
- *"Every hospital posts a greeting. None of them tells me anything."*
- *"I did not want to ask the doctor because it felt like a religious question."*

Beneath these: **a predictable period is about to change how people manage their health,
and nobody addresses it until it has already gone wrong.**

## Human Insight

**The occasion is not the content. The occasion is the reason the content is finally
being read.**

People do not want a greeting from a hospital. They want to know what to do about their
tablets during Ramadan, whether their father should be fasting, and why their mother
becomes confused in the heat. Those questions exist every year, are entirely
predictable, and are answered nowhere.

The empty greeting is worse than absence: it takes the moment of highest attention and
spends it on nothing, which teaches the reader that this account has nothing to offer
when it matters.

> Would a reader think *"how did they know that?"* — yes. Everyone managing a condition
> through Ramadan has improvised, and every institution they follow posted a lantern.

The second half, particular to this market: **religious and cultural occasions are not
marketing opportunities and are felt as such immediately.** A hospital that uses Ramadan
to be visible is noticed doing it. A hospital that uses Ramadan to answer the medication
question is doing what the occasion actually calls for — and the difference is entirely
whether the post contains anything.

## Invisible Product

- **An answer at the moment it is needed**, rather than after.
- **Permission to ask a question that feels religious** — the fasting-with-a-condition
  question, which people avoid raising with clinicians.
- **Something to send to a relative** who is about to do something risky.
- **Presence that is not decorative.**

## Psychological Transformation

**Before:** *"Everyone posts greetings. I will work out the medication thing myself."*

**After:** *"They actually answered the question. That is the account I check when the
season changes something."*

From **decorative presence to useful presence.**

## Emotional Strategy

**Primary: usefulness** — the recognition that this post exists to help rather than to
appear.

Supporting: warmth, respect, relief, belonging.

Never: fear about a season, seasonal urgency, or the greeting-card register. And never
anything that instructs on religious observance — see *Boundaries*.

## Audience

**Primary — people managing a condition through the period.** Diabetes, hypertension,
kidney disease, pregnancy, older adults. The most affected and least served.

**Primary — the family member responsible for them.** Usually the one who will act on the
content.

**Secondary — the general public**, for whom the seasonal health point is genuinely
interesting.

**Supporting — physicians**, who will judge whether the clinical content is sound and
appropriately bounded.

## Audience Decision

**What they fear:** getting it wrong during a period when their doctor is less
accessible; being told not to observe something they intend to observe.

**What actually moves them:** content that respects the intention and addresses the
health question inside it. *"If you are going to fast, this is what to discuss with your
doctor about your medication"* is used. *"People with diabetes should not fast"* is
ignored and resented.

# ═══════════ MEDICAL FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Service Boundaries

- This campaign does **not** give religious rulings, or advise anyone whether to observe
  a religious practice. Ever.
- It does **not** tell anyone with a condition whether they may fast. It says what to
  discuss with their clinician.
- It does **not** give individual medication timing advice — only the general principle
  and the instruction to consult.
- It does **not** use a season to create fear or urgency.
- It does **not** publish a greeting with no health content.
- It does **not** promote a service, screening or offer during a season.
- It does **not** use a religious or national occasion for brand visibility.

## Medical Philosophy

Seasonal periods produce predictable clinical patterns, and predictable patterns are
addressable in advance. This campaign is prevention with a calendar attached.

The clinical rule that governs everything here: **the campaign never adjudicates between
a person's condition and their intention.** It describes what changes physiologically,
names what needs medical input, and defers. Anything else is both clinically unsafe and
culturally presumptuous.

**Clinical review is mandatory**, and for seasonal content it should happen well before
the period begins.

## Core Principles

**The occasion is the timing; the content is the content.** No content, no post.

**Never instruct on observance.** Describe the physiology, name the medical question,
defer to the clinician.

**Respect the intention.** Content written for someone who is going to fast is used;
content telling them not to is not.

**Plan early.** Seasonal content cannot be produced retrospectively.

**No fear.** A season is not a threat.

**No promotion.** An occasion is not a sales window.

**Clinical review before the season starts.**

## Core Features

The periods available, and what each one actually changes. Specific clinical content
requires a clinician.

**Ramadan** — the largest and most valuable territory in the Egyptian calendar.
- Medication timing for twice- and thrice-daily regimens.
- Fluid management, particularly for kidney patients and older adults.
- Diabetes management during fasting hours.
- Sleep disruption and its effects.
- Iftar patterns and blood-sugar spikes.
- The question people avoid asking, in every case: *should I be fasting at all* — which
  the campaign frames as a question for their clinician and never answers.

**Eid**
- Diet after a month of altered eating.
- Travel, and medication carried or forgotten.
- Older relatives visited after a gap, and what family members notice.

**Summer heat**
- Dehydration in older adults and its presentation as confusion.
- Heat and chronic conditions.
- Medication storage in high temperatures — rarely mentioned, genuinely important.
- What changes in emergency presentations, where the operator confirms it.

**The first cold week**
- Respiratory conditions, and what changes for people managing them.
- The recurring myth that cold weather itself causes illness — routed to *Medical Myths &
  Facts*, not answered here.

**Exam season**
- Sleep, stimulants and stress in adolescents.
- The family's role, which is where the content is actually read.

**School return**
- Children's illness patterns, and what is ordinary.

**Awareness dates**
- Used only as timing, never as subject. If the post would not be worth reading on an
  ordinary day, the date did not make it worth reading.

## Differentiators

**Every post carries health content.** The category norm is the greeting.

**It respects the intention rather than adjudicating it.** Which is what makes fasting
content usable.

**It addresses the question people will not ask their doctor.**

**It is planned rather than reactive** — which is also its main operational constraint.

## What Makes This Different

For a worker reading nothing else: **say what the season changes physiologically, name
what needs a doctor, respect what the reader intends to do, and never post a greeting
that says nothing.**

# ═══════════ MARKETING INTELLIGENCE ═══════════

## Communication Objective

To answer the question the season creates.

The test: does the post contain something the reader could not have got from a greeting?

## What We Are Really Selling

**Usefulness at the right moment.**

The visible content is seasonal health guidance. What is being sold is the demonstration
that this account is present when something changes — which is a claim about the
institution's character made at the moment attention is highest.

## Human Truth

**People remember who was useful when something changed.**

A second truth: **occasions expose motive.** Everything published during a religious or
national period is read as either service or self-promotion, and the audience makes the
distinction instantly.

## Audience Psychology

- **Everyone posts a greeting and nobody says anything.** Universally observed.
- **They are using the occasion.** The default suspicion during religious periods.
- **I will not ask the doctor about fasting; he will just tell me not to.** Which is why
  the question goes unasked.
- **The seasonal advice is always the same.** Because it is usually generic.

## Emotional Triggers

**Primary:** usefulness. **Supporting:** warmth, respect, relief.
Excluded: fear, urgency, festivity for its own sake, and religious sentiment used as
tone.

## Psychological Barriers

**Barrier 1 — "This is just a greeting."** Corrected by content, always.
**Barrier 2 — "They are using Ramadan to advertise."** Corrected by never promoting
during an occasion.
**Barrier 3 — "They will tell me not to fast."** Corrected by respecting intention and
deferring the decision.
**Barrier 4 — "This does not apply to me."** Corrected by naming who each point is for.

## Core Messaging Framework

- Here is what this period actually changes.
- Here is the question to take to your doctor, before it starts.
- If you are going to do this, here is what to plan for.
- We are not telling you whether to observe anything.
- Nothing here replaces your own clinician.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. **What the period changes**, physiologically.
2. **Who it affects most.**
3. **What to plan or discuss**, before it begins.
4. **The deferral** to the clinician.
5. **The greeting**, if at all, last and brief.

Never open with the greeting. If there is only one line, it is what the period changes.

## Narrative Themes

- **What Ramadan changes about your tablets.** The single most valuable seasonal post
  available to this ecosystem.
- **The question to ask before the month starts.**
- **If you are going to fast.** Respecting intention.
- **Heat and confusion in older people.**
- **Where you keep your medication in summer.**
- **After the month** — Eid, and returning to a routine.
- **The relative you see once a year**, and what people notice.
- **Exam season sleep**, addressed to the family.

## Content Pillars

**1. What This Period Changes** · **2. Managing A Condition Through It** ·
**3. Ask Before It Starts** · **4. For The Family** · **5. Coming Out Of It**

## Storytelling Opportunities

- A tablet box and a Ramadan timetable, side by side.
- The pre-Ramadan conversation with a doctor, described as routine.
- A glass of water at suhoor, and why it matters for kidney patients.
- Medication left in a car in August.
- The relative visited at Eid, and the changes a year makes visible.
- A student at 3am in exam season, addressed to their parent.

## Communication Style

Always: useful, warm, respectful, specific, brief.

Never: festive for its own sake, decorative, alarmist, or preachy.

## Tone of Voice

Write as a doctor who knows the period matters to people and is helping them do it
safely.

Never instruct on observance. Never use religious phrasing as decoration. Never use:
*this holy month reminds us*, *as we celebrate*, *warmest wishes from all of us* — as the
substance of a post.

## Messaging Rules

- Always carry health content; never publish an empty greeting.
- Always respect the reader's intention.
- Always defer the decision to the clinician.
- Always plan and review before the period begins.
- Never give a religious ruling or advise on observance.
- Never tell anyone with a condition whether they may fast.
- Never use fear or seasonal urgency.
- Never promote a service during an occasion.
- Never let this campaign take *Hospital Life*'s material — Ramadan on a ward is that
  campaign.

# ═══════════ OFFERS ═══════════

## Current Offers

**No offers.** Seasonal promotion is the most common and most resented use of an
occasion, and `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §8 rules out discounts as an attraction
mechanism in any case.

## Offer Rules

- No offer, price, package or seasonal promotion. Ever.
- No screening or check-up campaign attached to an occasion.
- A Ramadan or Eid post that ends in a promotion is the single worst available use of
  this campaign.

## Call To Action

1. **Ask your doctor before it starts** — the most useful CTA in the campaign, and a
   deferral rather than a sale.
2. **None.**
3. **Send this to whoever needs it** — seasonal content is forwarded within families more
   than any other kind.

Never a booking CTA.

## Primary KPI

**Shares.** Seasonal health content travels within families, which is exactly the
distribution the campaign needs. Saves are secondary; reach is not the measure.

# ═══════════ STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE ═══════════

## Strategic Reminder

Before publishing, ask: **does this contain anything a greeting would not?**

If not, do not publish. An empty seasonal post spends the year's highest attention on
nothing.

## Strategic Constraints

- Never adjudicate religious observance.
- Never promote during an occasion.
- Never use fear about a season.
- Never publish seasonal content without clinical review, arranged in advance.
- Never take *Hospital Life*'s material.
- Long-term brand value outranks short-term engagement.

## Medical Communication Principles

- Every post clinically reviewed, before the period begins.
- No individual medication timing advice; general principle and deferral only.
- Never advise for or against fasting for anyone with a condition.
- Physiological explanation is permitted; personal adjudication is not.
- Where a seasonal claim is uncertain, omit it.

## Never Promise

- That any seasonal adjustment is safe for a given individual.
- That fasting is safe or unsafe for anyone.
- Any outcome from following seasonal guidance.
- Any figure about seasonal presentations not approved by the operator.
- Availability, staffing or waiting times during a period.

## Can Promise

- That the guidance is medically sound, clinically reviewed and general.
- That the decision belongs to the reader and their clinician.
- That education precedes promotion here (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5).
- That the ecosystem does not use occasions to sell.

## Brand Perception

**"They actually told me something useful about Ramadan instead of posting a lantern."**

## Long-Term Brand Contribution

Seasonal moments are the highest-attention windows in the year, and almost every
institution wastes them. Using them well, repeatedly, builds a specific and durable
expectation: that this account is useful when something changes.

Ramadan content in particular compounds — it is annual, predictable, and the same
questions arrive every year, which makes a well-built library of it permanently valuable.

## Relationship With INSAN

**Trust platform: Expertise.**

Seasonal guidance is identical across both hospitals and runs naturally on all three
pages. Branding should be light and promotion absent: the ecosystem's presence during an
occasion is credible only when it is not being used.

# ═══════════ INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE ═══════════

## Institutional Knowledge Extensions

**The system cannot currently plan for this campaign, and that is a real blocker.**
Audit B recorded that nothing in Campaign OS knows a season is approaching (B10), and the
proposed Egyptian events calendar (improvement I5) is not built. Seasonal content cannot
be produced retrospectively — the clinical review, the material and the timing all have
to be arranged before the period begins. **Until I5 exists, Ramadan and Eid content must
be planned manually and early**, and whoever owns the calendar should treat that as a
standing task rather than an annual surprise.

**Ramadan is the single most valuable content territory in the Egyptian year for a
healthcare organisation**, and it is almost entirely unserved. The medication-timing
question alone affects a large population with chronic conditions, recurs annually, and
is answered nowhere. One well-built Ramadan library would be worth more than the rest of
this campaign combined.

**The observance boundary is absolute and protects everyone.** A hospital that tells
people whether to fast has taken a religious position it has no standing for, will be
resented, and will lose the readers who most need the health content. Describing the
physiology and deferring the decision serves the reader better and keeps the institution
in its own domain.

**The Hospital Life boundary is the one most likely to be crossed.** Ramadan on a ward —
iftar in a staff room, a family sharing dates in a waiting area — is *Hospital Life*.
This campaign is what Ramadan does to a reader's health. Both are good posts; they are
different campaigns, and running them together produces two campaigns that look
identical every spring.

**What this file cannot supply.** The clinical content, and any local data about seasonal
presentation patterns. Both require a clinician and the operator.
