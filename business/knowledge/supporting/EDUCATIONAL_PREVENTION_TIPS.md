# ═══════════ FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Overview

Prevention Tips is for people who already know what they should do and are not doing it.

Its reader is not ignorant and does not need convincing. The gap is between knowing and
acting, and that gap is the campaign's entire subject.

## Definition

An educational campaign that makes a known preventive behaviour easier to actually do —
by making it smaller, attaching it to something the reader already does, or removing the
obstacle in front of it.

**What is inside it:** small, specific, doable versions of preventive behaviour;
attachment of a new behaviour to an existing routine; removal of a practical obstacle;
what to do rather than what to achieve; and the honest cases where a behaviour is harder
than health advice usually admits.

**What is outside it:**

- **A wrong belief.** *Medical Myths & Facts*.
- **Explaining a condition.** *Health Awareness*.
- **Seasonal relevance.** *Seasonal Campaigns*.
- **Screening or check-up promotion.** That is a service, and this campaign never
  promotes one.
- **Anything individual** — a plan, a diet, a regimen for a reader.

### The educational routing test

| If the reader's state is… | It belongs to |
|---|---|
| they believe something that is wrong | Medical Myths & Facts |
| they do not know something | Health Awareness |
| **they know but do not act** | **Prevention Tips** |
| the moment makes it relevant now | Seasonal Campaigns |

## Vision

That the ecosystem becomes the source that gives advice small enough to be taken.

## Mission

Close the gap between knowing and doing — one behaviour, made smaller.

## Why This Service Exists

**Prevention advice is the most given and least followed content in existence, and
giving more of it changes nothing.**

Everybody knows they should exercise. Everybody knows about salt, sugar, smoking and
sleep. The information gap closed decades ago; what remains is an execution gap, and it
is not addressed by repeating the information more attractively.

Health content continues to be written as though the barrier were knowledge because that
is the easy content to produce. It performs adequately, changes nothing, and gradually
teaches the audience that health advice is background noise.

This campaign exists to attack the actual barrier. `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5
commits the ecosystem to education before promotion; honest education here means
admitting that telling people is not the intervention.

## Business Objectives

- Produce content people actually act on, which is what earns a following worth having.
- Reduce presentation-stage severity through behaviours that are genuinely adopted.
- Establish the ecosystem as a useful rather than a hectoring source.
- Reach the audience that has stopped reading health advice.

## Marketing Objectives

- Make each behaviour small enough to be done today.
- Attach behaviours to existing routines rather than asking for new ones.
- Never lecture, and never imply the reader has been failing.
- Ask for nothing.

## Positioning

For everyone who already knows what they should be doing and is not doing it, Prevention
Tips makes one thing small enough to actually start — where competing content repeats
advice the reader has heard for years, this one addresses why it has not happened.

> *Test applied:* a competitor can publish *"exercise thirty minutes a day"*. Publishing
> the version that acknowledges why that has not happened, and offers something smaller,
> requires taking the reader's life seriously.

## Brand Philosophy

**Education before promotion**, and **never guilt**.
`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §8 forbids emotional manipulation; §21 forbids fear. Guilt
is the default register of prevention advice and it is closed here — it produces
avoidance, not behaviour.

## Core Promise

**Everything we suggest is medically sound, clinically reviewed, small enough to be
done, and offered without judgement.**

# ═══════════ HUMAN UNDERSTANDING ═══════════

## Human Problem

- *"I know. I know all of it. I'm not doing it."*
- *"I don't have time to exercise."*
- *"Every article says the same thing."*
- *"I tried and stopped after a week."*
- *"Healthy food is expensive."*
- *"I feel guilty every time I read one of these."*

Beneath these: **the reader has already been told, has already tried, and is now being
told again by someone who does not appear to know that.**

## Human Insight

**Advice fails because it asks for a new habit. What works is attaching to an existing
one.**

Nobody has a slot in their day for a new behaviour. Every hour is already occupied, and
a suggestion to add thirty minutes of exercise is a suggestion to remove thirty minutes
of something else — which the reader will not do, and knows they will not do while
reading.

What does work is attachment: a behaviour bound to something that already happens
reliably. The tablet next to the toothbrush. The walk that is the route to somewhere you
were going anyway. The water glass filled when the kettle is on. No new time is
requested, so no trade is required.

> Would a reader think *"how did they know that?"* — yes. Everyone has read health
> advice while knowing they would not follow it, and no health campaign has ever been
> written as though that were the normal outcome.

The second half, which is a tone rule as much as a method: **the reader has not been
failing. The advice was too big.** Prevention content is written with an undertone of
disappointment, and the reader hears it. Removing the judgement is not politeness — it is
what allows the suggestion to be considered at all.

## Invisible Product

- **Relief from guilt.** The dominant emotion this category produces, and the one worth
  removing.
- **One thing to start**, instead of a programme.
- **Permission to start small** without it counting as failure.
- **Agency** — something doable today.

## Psychological Transformation

**Before:** *"I know what I should be doing. I am not doing it, and reading about it
makes me feel worse."*

**After:** *"That one is small enough. I could do that tomorrow, and I do not have to
change anything else."*

From **guilt to a first step.**

## Emotional Strategy

**Primary: relief** — from the pressure the category normally applies.

Supporting: competence, agency, warmth, mild humour.

Never: guilt, shame, fear, urgency, or the disappointed-doctor register. And never the
transformation framing — before-and-after bodies, discipline, willpower — which is both
manipulative and, in a healthcare context, unkind.

## Audience

**Primary — adults who already know.** Which is nearly everyone; this is the campaign
with the broadest possible reader.

**Primary — the household health decision-maker**, who is managing other people's
behaviour as well as their own and is the most exhausted reader in the category.

**Secondary — people with a condition to manage**, for whom small adherence changes
matter most.

**Supporting — physicians**, who judge whether the advice is sound and proportionate.

## Audience Decision

**What they fear:** being lectured; being made to feel they have failed; starting and
stopping again.

**What actually moves them:** a suggestion small enough that failing at it is not
plausible. The threshold is doability, not importance — and importance is what health
content optimises for.

# ═══════════ MEDICAL FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Service Boundaries

- This campaign does **not** give individual advice, plans, diets or regimens.
- It does **not** advise on medication, supplements, dose or brand.
- It does **not** address a contested nutritional or lifestyle question.
- It does **not** advise anyone with a condition to change their treatment; every such
  post defers to their clinician.
- It does **not** promote screening, check-ups or any service.
- It does **not** use guilt, fear or body-image framing.
- It does **not** publish a figure or study the operator has not approved.

## Medical Philosophy

Prevention is the highest-value clinical activity available and the least deliverable by
communication. Honesty about that is what keeps this campaign useful: the intervention is
not the information, it is the reduction in effort required.

**Clinical review is mandatory** — including for advice that seems obvious, because
"obvious" prevention advice is frequently wrong for people with common conditions.

## Core Principles

**Make it smaller.** Smaller than feels worth publishing. That is the point.

**Attach, do not add.** Bind the behaviour to something already reliable.

**Never imply failure.** The reader has not been failing.

**One behaviour per post.**

**Never promote a service.** Prevention advice that arrives at a check-up package is an
advertisement.

**Defer for anyone with a condition.**

**Clinical review, every post.**

## Core Features

The territories available. Specific advice requires clinical authorship and review.

- **Movement, made small** — the walk that is a route, the stairs that are already
  there. Never a programme.
- **Salt, sugar and portion**, framed as a single swap rather than a diet.
- **Water**, attached to an existing trigger.
- **Sleep**, addressed as the obstacle rather than the target.
- **Medication adherence** — the highest-value behaviour in this category and the least
  covered; attachment works better here than anywhere.
- **Smoking**, handled with unusual care: the reader has been told, has tried, and does
  not need another reminder. The only honest content is about making one attempt easier.
- **Sitting**, for people who cannot exercise.
- **The obstacle itself** — cost, time, family resistance — addressed openly rather than
  assumed away.
- **What to do on a bad day** — the post that prevents an interruption becoming an
  ending.

## Differentiators

**It addresses the execution gap, not the information gap.** The category does the
opposite.

**Its advice is small enough to be embarrassing.** Which is why it works.

**It removes guilt deliberately.** The category's default register, and its main
obstacle.

**It never promotes a service.** Prevention content is the most common vehicle for
check-up promotion in this market.

**It takes obstacles seriously** — cost, time, exhaustion — instead of pretending they
are excuses.

## What Makes This Different

For a worker reading nothing else: **take one thing everyone already knows, make it
small enough to do tomorrow, attach it to something that already happens, and do not
suggest the reader has been failing.**

# ═══════════ MARKETING INTELLIGENCE ═══════════

## Communication Objective

To get one thing done.

The test: is the suggestion small enough that the reader could not plausibly fail at it?

## What We Are Really Selling

**A first step without a verdict.**

The visible content is a preventive suggestion. What is being sold is relief from the
judgement the category normally carries — and the resulting possibility that the reader
tries something instead of closing the page.

## Human Truth

**People do not act on what is important. They act on what is easy.**

A second truth: **being told again is experienced as being blamed.** Repetition in this
category is not neutral; it accumulates as guilt, and guilt produces avoidance.

## Audience Psychology

- **I already know all of this.** Correct.
- **This is telling me I have failed.** Usually the subtext, and always heard.
- **I do not have time.** True more often than health content admits.
- **I will start on Monday.** The permanent deferral this campaign is trying to
  interrupt.
- **They will end by selling me a check-up.** Frequently correct.

## Emotional Triggers

**Primary:** relief. **Supporting:** competence, agency, warmth, mild humour.
Excluded: guilt, shame, fear, urgency, discipline, transformation.

## Psychological Barriers

**Barrier 1 — "I already know this."** The defining barrier. Corrected by never being
about the information.
**Barrier 2 — "I do not have time."** Corrected by attachment rather than addition.
**Barrier 3 — "I have tried and failed."** Corrected by scale, and by the bad-day post.
**Barrier 4 — "This makes me feel worse."** Corrected by removing every trace of
judgement.
**Barrier 5 — "They want to sell me something."** Corrected by never promoting.

## Core Messaging Framework

- You already know. This is not about knowing.
- Here is a smaller version.
- Attach it to something you already do.
- A bad day is not the end of it.
- If you have a condition, your doctor's instruction comes first.
- We are not asking you to do anything else.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. **The acknowledgement** — you know this already.
2. **The smaller version.**
3. **The attachment** — what to bind it to.
4. **The bad-day clause.**
5. **The deferral**, where a condition is involved.

Never open with the benefit. If there is only one line, it is the smaller version.

## Narrative Themes

- **You already know.** The opening the category never uses.
- **Attach it to something.** The method, told as itself.
- **Too small to count.** Advice deliberately below the threshold that feels worth
  giving.
- **The bad day.** What to do when it breaks.
- **The obstacle, admitted.** Cost, time, exhaustion, taken seriously.
- **Not on Monday.** Interrupting the deferral.
- **The one that matters most** — medication adherence, made easy.

## Content Pillars

**1. Smaller Than That** · **2. Attach It** · **3. The Bad Day** ·
**4. The Real Obstacle** · **5. Taking It As Prescribed** · **6. If You Cannot Do The
Usual Advice**

## Storytelling Opportunities

- The tablet box next to the toothbrush.
- A walk that is a route to somewhere, not an exercise session.
- One swap in a meal, photographed as the meal.
- The stairs already in the building.
- The glass of water filled while the kettle boils.
- A week broken on day three, and what happens next.
- The honest post about cost, and what is actually possible on a budget.

## Communication Style

Always: warm, plain, small, specific, unhurried, judgement-free.

Never: motivational, disciplinary, aspirational, medicalised, or written by someone who
has clearly never missed a day.

## Tone of Voice

Write as a friend who happens to know medicine and is not disappointed in you.

Egyptian colloquial where the campaign is in Arabic. Short. Never *"you should"* — say
*"if you want a smaller version of this"*.

Never use: *commit to*, *transform*, *take control of your health*, *no excuses*,
*discipline*, *your journey starts today*.

## Messaging Rules

- Always acknowledge that the reader already knows.
- Always make the behaviour smaller than feels publishable.
- Always attach it to an existing routine.
- Always include the bad-day clause where relevant.
- Always defer to the clinician for anyone with a condition.
- Always have a clinician review it.
- Never use guilt, shame or fear.
- Never promote a screening, check-up or service.
- Never give an individual plan, diet or regimen.
- Never use body-image or transformation framing.
- Never address a contested nutritional question.

# ═══════════ OFFERS ═══════════

## Current Offers

**No offers.** Prevention content is the most common vehicle for check-up promotion in
this market, and using it that way is what has made the category untrusted.

## Offer Rules

- No offer, price or package.
- **No screening or check-up may be promoted here**, even where the ecosystem offers
  one. If a post would benefit from mentioning a service, the post is in the wrong
  campaign.

## Call To Action

1. **None**, or the behaviour itself — *"try the smaller one tomorrow"*.
2. **Tell us what gets in the way** — makes the obstacle visible and supplies the next
   post.
3. **Talk to your doctor**, where a condition is involved — a deferral, not a CTA.

Never a booking CTA.

## Primary KPI

**Saves, and comments describing an obstacle.**

Saves indicate intent to act. Obstacle comments are the campaign's most valuable output:
they are the execution gap described by the people living in it, and they should be
collected as input rather than only answered.

# ═══════════ STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE ═══════════

## Strategic Reminder

Before publishing, ask: **could the reader plausibly fail at this?**

If yes, it is too big. Make it smaller.

And: **would a reader who has tried and stopped feel judged by this?** If so, rewrite the
tone before the content.

## Strategic Constraints

- Never use guilt, shame or fear.
- Never promote a service.
- Never publish without clinical review.
- Never give individual advice.
- Never address a contested lifestyle question.
- Long-term brand value outranks short-term engagement.

## Medical Communication Principles

- Every post clinically reviewed, including the obvious advice — obvious prevention
  advice is frequently wrong for people with common conditions.
- No individual plans, diets, regimens or supplement advice.
- Anyone with a condition is deferred to their clinician, explicitly.
- No medication advice beyond adherence to what was prescribed.
- Settled evidence only.

## Never Promise

- That any behaviour prevents, cures or protects against a disease.
- Any health outcome from following the suggestion.
- That the advice suits the reader's circumstances.
- Any figure, study finding or risk reduction not approved.
- Weight, appearance or performance outcomes.

## Can Promise

- That the suggestion is medically sound and clinically reviewed.
- That it is small, specific and asks nothing further.
- That education precedes promotion here (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5).
- That anyone with a condition should follow their own clinician first.

## Brand Perception

**"They give advice you can actually do, and they don't make you feel bad. That's rare."**

## Long-Term Brand Contribution

This campaign builds the relationship that makes a healthcare page worth following
between crises — the everyday usefulness that keeps an audience present when they are
well.

Its deeper contribution is trust of an unusual kind: a source that does not lecture is
believed when it eventually says something serious.

## Relationship With INSAN

**Trust platform: Expertise.**

A shared standard for what may be published as health advice, applied across three
pages, is a platform function. Branding should be light: prevention advice wearing a
hospital logo reads as marketing for the hospital, and the absence of an ask is this
campaign's credibility.

# ═══════════ INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE ═══════════

## Institutional Knowledge Extensions

**The cost obstacle is real here and should not be written around.** Advice that assumes
disposable income — specific foods, gym membership, supplements — excludes a large part
of the catchment and signals who the ecosystem thinks it is speaking to. The honest
version of this campaign takes cost seriously and says what is possible without it.
Getting this wrong is a positioning error, not only a content one.

**Medication adherence is the highest-value territory and the least used.** It is
unglamorous, it applies to everyone with a chronic condition, and the attachment method
works better there than anywhere. It should be a recurring subject rather than an
occasional one.

**Smoking requires the most restraint in the campaign.** Every smoker has been told, has
usually tried, and is the most lectured reader in health communication. The only honest
content is about making one attempt easier — never another reminder of the risk, which
is a fear breach as well as useless.

**Clinical review covers the obvious advice too.** Salt restriction, exercise and fasting
advice are all wrong for some common conditions, and the "obvious" post is the one most
likely to skip review.

**What this file cannot supply.** The advice itself, and which behaviours matter most in
this catchment. Both require a clinician and local knowledge.
