# Purpose

This document is the Source of Truth for the **FAQ** campaign.

It describes a campaign, not a medical entity. Where a medical service file answers
*what is this department*, this file answers *what is this campaign, what belongs
inside it, and what must never appear in it.*

Three scheduled posts depend on it.

# Document Philosophy

Written for a worker that has never seen INSAN and cannot ask. Everything here is
derived from `MASTER_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`,
`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md`, `PROJECT_DECISIONS.md` and the family-communication
material in `departments/MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`.

**This campaign looks like the least strategic in the plan and is one of the most
useful.** Its entire value depends on one insight, in *Human Insight*. Read it before
writing anything.

# ═══════════ FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Overview

FAQ answers the questions people actually ask — about attending, waiting, staying,
paying, bringing someone, finding something out.

It is the most practical campaign in the plan and the only one whose success is
measured in a problem removed rather than a belief changed.

## Definition

A supporting campaign that answers recurring practical questions about using the
ecosystem, one question per post, in the questioner's own words.

**What is inside it:** how to attend, book or reach a service; what happens on arrival;
visiting and staying with a patient; what to bring; who to ask about what; how costs
and insurance are handled in principle; how to get a report, a result or a document;
what a family can expect to be told, and when.

**What is outside it:**

- **Preparation for a specific event.** *What to do the week before your operation* is
  a sequence, not an answer — that is *Patient Guides*.
- **Medical or health questions.** *Is this symptom serious?* is clinical, and belongs
  to *Educational* content or to a consultation. This campaign never answers it.
- **Myths and misconceptions.** *Medical Myths & Facts* is a separate campaign with a
  different purpose: it corrects a belief, this one removes an obstacle.
- **How a process works internally.** Sterilisation, handover, audit — *Behind The
  Scenes*.
- **The experience of being there.** *Hospital Life*.
- Anything that would require a clinical judgement about an individual.

### The routing test for practical campaigns

| If the reader's need is… | It belongs to |
|---|---|
| **a single recurring question with a short answer** | **FAQ** |
| a sequence to follow before or after an event | Patient Guides |
| a belief to correct | Medical Myths & Facts |
| health knowledge to acquire | Health Awareness / Prevention Tips |

If the answer takes more than a short paragraph, it is probably a Guide. If it starts
with *"actually, no —"*, it is Myths & Facts.

## Vision

That nobody delays care, arrives unprepared, or spends a day in a corridor because
something obvious was never written down.

## Mission

Answer the real question underneath the asked one, plainly, and in the words the
questioner used.

## Why This Service Exists

**Practical uncertainty is a clinical problem.**

People delay attending because they do not know what it will involve. They arrive
without a document and lose a day. They do not ask whether they can stay with their
mother overnight, so they leave when they are told visiting has ended. They avoid
asking about cost until it is too late to plan.

None of this is a medical failure and all of it changes outcomes.
`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` records the same conclusion from the ICU's side:
administrative stress during an emotional crisis is a real burden, and reducing
unnecessary administrative friction is part of the care.

There is a second reason, and it is the strategic one. **Answering a question publicly
is a transparency behaviour.** `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3 names radical
transparency — total clarity about procedures, costs and alternatives — as an
ecosystem advantage. A campaign that answers awkward practical questions in public
demonstrates that value more cheaply and more convincingly than any statement about it.

And a third, which is easy to miss: **the questions themselves are data.** What people
ask reveals where the institution is confusing, and a question that recurs is an
operational defect wearing a content shape.

## Business Objectives

- Reduce the practical barriers that cause people to delay or avoid attending.
- Reduce avoidable friction at reception, admissions and on the phone.
- Demonstrate transparency in the cheapest and most credible available form.
- Surface recurring confusions so they can be fixed operationally rather than answered
  repeatedly.
- Build the everyday usefulness that keeps an audience following a healthcare page
  between crises.

## Marketing Objectives

- Answer the real question, not the literal one.
- Establish the ecosystem as an organisation that tells you things without being
  chased.
- Give the audience a reason to save and share posts, which practical content earns
  more reliably than any other kind.
- Make asking feel permitted — which changes behaviour inside the building, not only
  online.

## Positioning

For anyone who has a practical question about a hospital and expects to have to chase
someone for the answer, FAQ answers it in public, in advance — where competing pages
publish services and achievements, this one publishes the answers people are actually
searching for, including the awkward ones.

> *Test applied:* a competitor can publish opening hours. Publishing *"can I stay
> overnight with my mother, and what happens if I am told I cannot"* requires a settled
> position on the question and the willingness to be held to it.

## Brand Philosophy

**Communication is part of the care, not a service around it.**

`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` states it directly: silence creates fear, communication
creates trust. This campaign is the least dramatic form of that philosophy and one of
the most frequently useful.

`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §8 requires clear, brief, calm and human messaging. FAQ
is the campaign where the tone requirement and the content requirement are the same
thing.

## Core Promise

**We will answer the question as asked, honestly, including when the answer is no —
and we will tell you who to ask when we cannot answer it here.**

The promise is about the honesty and completeness of the answer. It says nothing about
what the answers will be.

# ═══════════ HUMAN UNDERSTANDING ═══════════

## Human Problem

What the person is actually living through:

- *"I don't want to call and sound stupid."*
- *"I asked at reception and they told me to ask someone else."*
- *"I took the day off and they said I needed a document nobody mentioned."*
- *"Am I allowed to stay with her? Nobody said, and I didn't want to ask."*
- *"How much is this going to cost? I need to know before, not after."*
- *"Who do I even ask about this?"*
- *"I've been putting it off because I don't know what it involves."*

Beneath these: **people are more willing to go without an answer than to risk looking
ignorant or being a nuisance.** That is the mechanism this campaign exists to break,
and it is why the answers must be published rather than made available.

## Human Insight

**The question people ask is not the question they are worried about.**

*"What are your visiting hours?"* is almost never a question about hours. It is *"will
I be able to stay with my mother, and if not, will she be alone all night?"*
*"Do I need an appointment?"* is *"will I be turned away after travelling here?"*
*"How much does it cost?"* is *"will there be a number at the end that I have not
prepared for?"*

An FAQ that answers the literal question is administratively correct and emotionally
useless. It closes the sentence and leaves the fear exactly where it was.

> Would a reader think *"how did they know that?"* — yes. Everybody has asked a
> narrow, safe version of the question they actually had, and been answered narrowly,
> and left still worried. Nobody has ever had an institution answer the real one.

The operating rule that follows, and the whole method of this campaign: **answer the
asked question in one line, then answer the real one.**

> *"Visiting is from 4 to 8. And if your mother is frightened or unwell, ask the nurse
> in charge — nobody is asked to leave a patient who needs them."*

The second sentence is the post. The first is the reason it gets read.

## Invisible Product

The visible product is an answer.

What the reader is really buying:

- **Permission to ask.** The largest effect this campaign has, and it operates inside
  the building rather than online.
- **Not being caught out.** The specific relief of knowing what to bring, when to
  arrive, what to expect.
- **The removal of a reason to delay.** Which is a clinical outcome, not a marketing
  one.
- **Evidence of how the institution behaves** — an organisation that answers awkward
  questions in public probably answers them in person.
- **Something to send to a relative.** Practical answers are the most forwarded content
  a healthcare page produces.

## Psychological Transformation

**Before:**
> *"I have questions I feel stupid asking, so I'll turn up and find out, or I'll put it
> off."*

**After:**
> *"They've answered it already, including the part I was too embarrassed to ask. And
> if I have another question, apparently asking is normal."*

The movement is from **hesitancy to permission** — and permission is what changes what
happens at the reception desk.

## Emotional Strategy

**Primary emotion: relief.**

Supporting: reassurance, competence, warmth, being taken seriously.

Never: condescension, impatience, institutional voice, or the register of a policy
being explained to someone who should already have known it. And never the cheerful
brand voice — an FAQ that is trying to be charming is not trying to be useful.

The register is a helpful, unhurried person who has answered this many times and does
not mind answering it again.

## Audience

**Primary — anyone with an upcoming or possible hospital visit.**
Patients, adult children arranging care for a parent, spouses. They are searching for
practical certainty and will read anything that provides it.

**Primary — people currently in the building.**
A large and invisible audience. People sitting in a waiting area with a phone in their
hand are among the most likely readers of this campaign, and the most immediately
served by it.

**Secondary — people who are delaying.**
Practical uncertainty is a real cause of avoidance. This campaign reaches them where a
health message would not.

**Secondary — staff.**
Reception, nursing and coordination staff answer these questions all day. A published
answer becomes the answer everyone gives, which is a consistency benefit inside the
organisation.

**Who decides, who influences, who pays:** the family member organising care reads most
and decides most; the patient is often the last to be told.

## Audience Decision

**What they compare:** nothing. This audience is not choosing an institution; they are
trying to use one. That makes FAQ the only supporting campaign whose reader is already
committed.

**What they fear:** wasting a journey, being turned away, being embarrassed, an
unexpected cost, being separated from their relative.

**Where they look:** search and the page itself, then a phone call they would rather
not make.

**What actually moves them:** completeness. An answer with a caveat and a named next
step — *"and if that does not apply to you, ask the coordinator at the desk"* — is
worth more than a cleaner answer that leaves an edge case dangling.

# ═══════════ MEDICAL FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Service Boundaries

- This campaign does **not** answer clinical questions, describe symptoms, or advise on
  whether someone should attend.
- It does **not** state prices, fees or rates.
- It does **not** state visiting hours, opening times, phone numbers or any operational
  detail the operator has not supplied and confirmed as current.
- It does **not** promise a waiting time, an availability or a bed.
- It does **not** give insurance answers specific to a policy or a provider.
- It does **not** answer a question about an individual's case.
- It does **not** publish an answer that differs between the two hospitals without
  saying which is which.

## Medical Philosophy

Information is part of care. A family that knows what to expect copes better, complies
better and arrives earlier — which `MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` records as an operating
conclusion rather than a communication theory.

The clinical boundary is absolute and simple: **this campaign answers questions about
using the institution, never about the body.** The moment a question is about a
symptom, a condition or whether to worry, the honest answer is that it needs a
clinician — and saying so is itself a good answer.

## Core Principles

**Answer the real question.** One line for the asked question, then the concern
underneath it. This is the campaign's entire method.

**One question per post.** A list of ten answers is a document, not a post, and is read
by nobody.

**Use their words.** The post's question should be phrased as a person would ask it,
including the awkwardness. *"Can I stay with my mum overnight?"* not *"What is the
visitor accommodation policy?"*

**Never state an operational detail we have not verified.** A wrong opening time is a
wasted journey for a real person, and it is the most damaging error this campaign can
make.

**Say no clearly.** An honest no with a reason and an alternative is a good answer. A
vague answer that avoids the no is worse than the no.

**Always name the next step.** Every answer ends with who to ask if the reader's case
is different. It costs one line and it removes the dead end.

**A recurring question is a defect.** If the same thing is asked repeatedly, the answer
is a post and the cause is an operations ticket. Record both.

## Core Features

The question territories available to this campaign. Specific answers must come from
the operator; these are the categories that recur in any hospital of this type and that
the committed documents support answering.

**Getting there and getting seen**
- How to book, and whether an appointment is required.
- What to bring — identification, referral, previous reports, imaging, medication list.
- Where to go on arrival, and who to speak to first.
- What happens if you arrive without something.
- Which services are at Future and which at Delta — the ecosystem question that most
  reliably confuses people.

**Waiting, staying and visiting**
- Visiting arrangements, and — the real question — whether a family member can stay.
- Whether a relative can accompany a patient into a consultation or a procedure area.
- Where families can wait, and what is available during a long wait.
- What happens overnight.

**Being told things**
- Who gives updates about a patient, and how often.
- Who to ask when nobody has said anything.
- How to get a medical report, a result or a copy of a file.
- What a family can expect to be told, and what requires the patient's consent.

**Money, in principle**
- How costs are discussed and when — `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3 and the ICU
  Financial Transparency Framework support answering the *process* question fully
  without quoting a price.
- How insurance and corporate contracts are handled in general terms.
- Who to speak to about payment, and when that conversation happens.
- The honest answer to *"will there be surprises?"* — which is the process, not a
  guarantee.

**Emergencies**
- When to come to an emergency department rather than a clinic — stated
  non-clinically, as a route question, never as triage advice.
- What happens on arrival at an emergency department.
- Whether a family member can accompany.

**Being in the ecosystem**
- What INSAN is, in one line, for the reader who noticed the name.
- Whether a file or a referral moves between the two hospitals.
- What a medical center is, and how it differs from a clinic.

## Differentiators

**It answers the real question.** Every institution publishes an FAQ; almost none
answers the concern underneath the question. This one difference is the whole campaign.

**It answers awkward questions.** Cost, staying overnight, being turned away, not being
told anything. Publishing these is a transparency behaviour that costs something, which
is why competitors avoid it.

**It uses the questioner's words.** FAQ pages are written in institutional language,
which is why they read as policy. Writing the question as a person would say it is
unusual enough to be noticed.

**It covers the ecosystem, not one building.** Which services are where, whether files
move — questions a single-hospital page cannot even have.

**Its questions are an operational feedback loop.** The campaign generates a list of
what confuses people, which is worth more internally than the posts are worth
externally.

## Service Journey

Where each question arises, which is also the publishing sequence:

1. **Before deciding** — is it worth going, what does it involve.
2. **Before travelling** — appointment, documents, where and when.
3. **On arrival** — where to go, who to speak to.
4. **During the wait** — how long, where to wait, who knows anything.
5. **During a stay** — visiting, staying, updates, who to ask.
6. **Around money** — when it is discussed, with whom.
7. **On leaving** — reports, results, follow-up, documents.

Steps 4 and 5 are where the strongest posts are, because they are where people are
least willing to ask out loud.

## What Makes This Different

For a worker reading nothing else: **take one question in a person's own words, answer
it in a line, then answer what they were really asking, and name who to ask if their
case is different.**

# ═══════════ MARKETING INTELLIGENCE ═══════════

## Communication Objective

To remove an obstacle.

Not to persuade, not to reassure and not to build image. The objective is that one
specific practical uncertainty stops existing for the reader — and the test is whether
they could now act without calling anyone.

## What We Are Really Selling

**Permission.**

The visible content is a practical answer. What is being sold is the message underneath
every post: **asking is normal here, and you will be told the truth.** That is a claim
about the institution's behaviour, delivered by demonstration rather than assertion,
and it transfers to every interaction the reader has afterwards.

Restated for a worker: we are not selling convenience or service. We are selling **the
expectation of being answered.**

## Human Truth

**People would rather go without information than risk looking foolish.**

Universal, rarely acknowledged, and the reason FAQ content works at all. Publishing the
answer removes the social cost of the question, which is the actual barrier — not the
absence of the information.

A second truth: **the most useful content is the most forwarded.** Practical answers
travel between family members in a way that no brand content does.

## Audience Psychology

How the audience thinks before anything is said:

- **Hospitals do not tell you things; you have to chase.** Learned from experience.
- **Asking marks you as ignorant.** Which suppresses the questions most worth asking.
- **The real answer is never the published one.** So a published answer with a caveat
  reads as more honest than a clean one.
- **Money will not be discussed until it is too late.** The most common and most
  corrosive expectation.
- **Nobody will tell me what is happening with my relative.** The specific fear behind
  the majority of questions during a stay.

## Emotional Triggers

**Primary:** relief.

**Supporting:** reassurance, gratitude, competence, warmth.

Deliberately excluded: cheerfulness, brand personality, humour at the questioner's
expense, and any register that implies the question was obvious.

## Psychological Barriers

**Barrier 1 — "They won't really answer."**
The expectation that published answers are evasive. Corrected by answering the awkward
questions first: the ones about cost, staying overnight and not being told anything.

**Barrier 2 — "It'll be different when I actually go."**
Corrected by accuracy, and by naming the next step for the cases the answer does not
cover. It is also corrected operationally, or not at all — a published answer the
building does not honour is worse than none.

**Barrier 3 — "I still don't want to ask."**
The social barrier persists even when the information exists. Corrected by phrasing the
question as a person would actually ask it, awkwardness included — which signals that
the awkward version is acceptable here.

**Barrier 4 — "This is for other people, not my situation."**
Corrected by always naming the next step: who to ask when your case is different.

## Core Messaging Framework

Consistently reinforced:

- Asking is normal and expected here.
- We will answer honestly, including when the answer is no.
- Cost is discussed at the beginning, not at the end.
- Families are expected, not tolerated.
- If this answer does not fit your case, here is who to ask.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. **The question**, in the reader's own words.
2. **The direct answer**, in one line.
3. **The real answer** — the concern underneath it.
4. **The exception** — what to do if your case is different, and who to ask.
5. **Nothing else.** No sign-off, no brand line, no CTA.

Never reverse it, and never open with the institution. If there is only one line, it is
the direct answer — but a post with only the direct answer is not this campaign.

## Narrative Themes

- **The question people are embarrassed to ask.** The campaign's strongest recurring
  format.
- **What nobody tells you before you come.**
- **Can I stay with them?** — the single most emotionally loaded practical question in
  a hospital.
- **When does anybody tell me anything?** — updates, and who owns them.
- **The money conversation** — when it happens, and with whom.
- **What to bring.** The most forwarded post type in this campaign.
- **Which hospital, which service.** The ecosystem question.
- **What happens when you arrive.** Removing the fear of the first ten minutes.
- **The honest no.** A question where the answer is not what the reader hoped, answered
  plainly with an alternative.

## Content Pillars

**1. Before You Come** — booking, documents, where and when.
**2. When You Arrive** — the first ten minutes.
**3. Staying And Visiting** — the questions families are least willing to ask.
**4. Being Told Things** — updates, reports, who to ask.
**5. Money, Explained As A Process** — when, with whom, and what to expect.
**6. Using The Ecosystem** — which service is where, and what moves between hospitals.

## Storytelling Opportunities

Not stories — the question itself is the format. What varies is which question:

- *"Can I stay the night with my mother?"*
- *"What do I need to bring with me?"*
- *"I don't have a referral. Will you still see me?"*
- *"Who do I ask when nobody has told me anything for hours?"*
- *"When will someone talk to me about the cost?"*
- *"Can I go in with him?"*
- *"How do I get a copy of the report?"*
- *"Should I go to emergency or wait for a clinic appointment?"* — answered as a route
  question, never as triage.
- *"Is this service at Future or at Delta?"*
- *"What is INSAN? I thought this was a hospital."*

## Communication Style

Always: plain, short, direct, warm, complete.

Never: institutional, apologetic, cheerful, promotional, or hedged into uselessness.

The test: **would a person read this and stop worrying?** Administratively correct and
still worried is a failed post.

## Tone of Voice

Write as the most helpful person on the reception desk — the one who answers the
question you asked, then adds the thing you actually needed to know, without making you
feel you should have known it.

Second person. Short sentences. Egyptian colloquial where the campaign is in Arabic,
because a question phrased formally is not the question anyone asks.

Never begin with *"At INSAN, we…"*. The reader has a question, not an interest in the
institution.

## Messaging Rules

- Always phrase the question as a person would ask it.
- Always answer the asked question first, in one line.
- Always answer the real concern underneath it.
- Always name who to ask when the answer does not fit.
- Always verify any operational detail before publishing.
- Never answer a clinical question, or imply whether a symptom is serious.
- Never state a price, a rate or a waiting time.
- Never publish an operational detail the operator has not confirmed.
- Never promise availability, a bed or a timeframe.
- Never make the reader feel the question was obvious.
- Never attach a promotional message to an answer.
- Never publish an answer the building will not honour.

# ═══════════ OFFERS ═══════════

## Current Offers

**No offers.** An FAQ answer that turns into a promotion breaks the only thing this
campaign has, which is being read as information rather than as marketing.

## Offer Rules

- No promotional offer, price, discount or package may appear in this campaign.
- Financial questions are answered as *process* — when the conversation happens and
  with whom — never as pricing, and never as a claim about being affordable.

## Call To Action

In priority order:

1. **Ask us anything** — the only CTA that belongs here, and the one that reinforces
   the campaign's actual product.
2. **None.** An answered question needs no ask.
3. **Contact the relevant desk** — where a real next step exists for cases the answer
   does not cover. This is part of the answer rather than a CTA.

Never a booking CTA on a practical answer. It converts help into a sales interaction
and teaches the reader that the help was conditional.

## Primary KPI

**Saves and shares.**

Practical content is saved for later and sent to relatives, and both behaviours mean
the post did its job. Comment volume is a weak signal here — a good answer produces no
discussion — but **new questions in the comments are the campaign's most valuable
output** and should be collected rather than merely replied to.

# ═══════════ STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE ═══════════

## Strategic Reminder

Before publishing, ask whether a person with this question could now act without
calling anyone.

If the answer is no, the post failed. If it answered the literal question but left the
worry in place, it also failed — that is the specific failure this campaign exists to
avoid.

## Strategic Constraints

- Never publish an operational detail that has not been verified as current.
- Never let an answer differ between the two hospitals without saying which is which.
- Never answer a clinical question, however easy it looks.
- Never allow a promotional message into an answer.
- An answer the institution will not honour is worse than no answer at all — this is
  the campaign's single largest risk.
- Long-term brand value outranks short-term engagement
  (`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §20.5).

## Medical Communication Principles

- No clinical advice, symptom assessment or triage guidance.
- Where a question is clinical, the answer is that it requires a clinician — stated
  respectfully, with who to contact.
- Answers about a patient's information must respect consent: what a family may be told
  is governed by the patient's wishes, and the answer should say so.
- Financial answers describe process, never amounts.
- Emergency-routing questions are answered as *where to go*, never as *how serious your
  symptom is*.

## Never Promise

The campaign must never promise:

- A waiting time, an appointment availability, or a bed.
- A price, a rate, a discount or insurance coverage.
- That a specific person will be available.
- That an exception will be granted — including the overnight-stay question, where the
  honest answer describes how the decision is made.
- Any clinical outcome or assessment.
- That an operational arrangement will not change.
- That every case is covered by the published answer.

## Can Promise

The campaign can confidently state:

- That questions are welcome and answering them is part of the service
  (`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`, family communication).
- That financial discussion happens early, with guidance on payment pathways
  (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3).
- That families are treated as partners in care rather than as visitors.
- That transparency about procedures, costs and alternatives is a stated operating
  value (§3, §7).
- That the same standard applies at both hospitals, because INSAN sets it.
- That where an answer does not fit a case, there is a named person to ask.

## Brand Perception

After sustained exposure, the audience should believe:

**"They actually answer things. If I ask, I'll get a real answer — including if it's
no."**

## Long-Term Brand Contribution

This campaign builds everyday usefulness, which is what keeps an audience following a
healthcare page in the long gaps between needing one.

It also produces the ecosystem's most durable content. A well-answered practical
question stays true and stays searched for years, and it is the content most likely to
be found by someone at the exact moment they need it.

Internally, it produces something more valuable than posts: a record of what confuses
people. A question that recurs is an operational defect, and this campaign is the only
one that surfaces them systematically.

## Relationship With INSAN

**Trust platform: Transparency.**

Answering practical questions in public — including the awkward ones — is transparency
practised at the smallest scale, and the scale is what makes it credible. A published
answer about cost or overnight stays does more for the transparency claim than any
statement about transparency.

Per `AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §16, this campaign runs naturally on all three pages.
Where an answer differs between hospitals it must say so, and where it is the same it
quietly demonstrates the platform: one answer, both buildings.

# ═══════════ INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE ═══════════

## Institutional Knowledge Extensions

**This file contains no answers, and that is deliberate.** Every actual answer —
visiting arrangements, required documents, who gives updates, how cost conversations
are scheduled — is an operational fact held by the two hospitals, and the two hospitals
may differ. A knowledge file that supplied them would be inventing operational
commitments about real institutions, and a wrong answer here costs a real person a
wasted day. **The operator must supply the answers before this campaign runs.**

**The overnight-stay question deserves a settled position before it is answered
publicly.** It is the single most emotionally loaded practical question in a hospital,
the answer is usually discretionary, and a published answer converts discretion into an
expectation. That is a decision for hospital management, not for a writer — and it is
worth making, because the question will be asked either way.

**The questions are worth more than the posts.** Whoever runs this campaign should keep
a list of every question that arrives in comments and messages. It is the cheapest
operational research the ecosystem has access to, and a recurring question is a defect
rather than a content opportunity.

**Why this campaign is not Patient Guides.** The boundary is length and shape: a
question with a short answer belongs here, a sequence to follow belongs there. Both
were scheduled separately and they will merge unless the routing test in *Definition*
is applied — the same convergence risk that the three experience campaigns carry.

## Knowledge Scope

This file covers the FAQ campaign: purpose, boundaries, audiences, method, tone and the

## AI Worker Guidance

**Apply the routing test before writing.** A sequence is a Guide. A correction is Myths
& Facts. A health topic is Educational.

**Common mistakes with this entity:**

- Answering only the literal question. The defining failure of this campaign.
- Writing the question in institutional language. Nobody asks *"what is the visitor
  accommodation policy"*.
- Inventing an operational detail — an hour, a document, a rule — to complete an
  answer. This is the error that costs a real person a real day, and it is the reason
  this file contains no answers.
- Answering a clinical question because it looked easy.
- Bundling ten questions into one post.
- Hedging until the answer is useless.
- Adding a promotional line or a booking CTA.

**When the strategy fields are blank**, refuse the row. And refuse the row when the
answer itself is not available — this campaign is the one where a plausible invention is
most immediately harmful.

## Maintenance Policy

Update when: an answer changes, an arrangement differs between the hospitals, or a new
question recurs often enough to be added to the territories.

**An answer that has changed is urgent.** A stale published answer is the one error in
this campaign that reaches a person before anyone notices.

Owner: INSAN Marketing & Brand Team, with hospital operations confirming every answer
before first publication and on any change.
