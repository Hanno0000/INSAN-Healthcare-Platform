# Purpose

This document is the Source of Truth for the **Community Impact (CSR)** campaign.

It describes a campaign, not a medical entity. Where a medical service file answers
*what is this department*, this file answers *what is this campaign, what belongs
inside it, and what must never appear in it.*

Three scheduled posts depend on it.

# Document Philosophy

Written for a worker that has never seen INSAN and cannot ask. Everything here is
derived from `MASTER_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`,
`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` and `PROJECT_DECISIONS.md`.

**This campaign is the only one in the plan whose publication changes what it is
publishing.** Charitable work that is announced is received differently from charitable
work that is done. Read *Human Insight* before writing anything — in this market the
problem is sharper than it is elsewhere.

# ═══════════ FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Overview

Community Impact covers what the ecosystem does for the community beyond treating the
patients who arrive: health outreach, screening in underserved places, education for
schools and workplaces, partnerships with local organisations, and support during
public need.

It is the campaign most likely to be read as reputation management, and the entire
craft of running it is avoiding that reading.

## Definition

A supporting campaign that documents the ecosystem's work in the community — with the
community as the subject and the ecosystem as the participant.

**What is inside it:** outreach and screening activity outside the hospital walls;
health education delivered to schools, workplaces or community organisations;
partnerships with local bodies; contribution during public health need; and — most
valuably — **what was learned about the community's health from doing it.**

**What is outside it:**

- **Individual charitable acts toward a patient.** Waiving a fee, funding a treatment,
  helping a family. **These are never published.** See *Human Insight*.
- **Anything involving an identifiable beneficiary who is a patient.**
- **General health education with no community activity behind it.** That is
  *Health Awareness* or *Prevention Tips*.
- **Staff volunteering as a culture story.** That is *Hospital Life* if it is about the
  place, or *Meet Our Team* if it is about a person.
- **The ecosystem's clinical work.** Treating patients is the service, not community
  impact.
- Sponsorship, awards, ceremonies and photocalls of any kind.

### The routing test

| If the post is fundamentally about… | It belongs to |
|---|---|
| **health activity in the community, and what it found** | **Community Impact** |
| health knowledge with no activity behind it | Health Awareness / Prevention Tips |
| a person on the staff | Meet Our Team |
| the culture of the institution | Hospital Life |

If the post's real subject is *how good the hospital is*, it is not this campaign
whatever it describes.

## Vision

That the ecosystem is understood as part of the health of its area rather than as a
building people are taken to — and that this is believed because it was demonstrated,
not because it was claimed.

## Mission

Do the work for the community's sake, and report it in a way that serves the community
rather than the reporter.

## Why This Service Exists

**A hospital's catchment is a population, not a customer base.**

Most of the health of the area around Future and Delta is determined outside their
walls: by what people know, what they screen for, when they present, and whether they
present at all. A hospital that only treats what arrives is passive in its own
catchment.

The ecosystem's stated position makes this more than optional.
`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5 puts *Education Before Promotion* among the permanent
strategic differentiators, and §3 defines continuity of care as beginning before the
visit. Both statements commit the ecosystem to activity outside the building, and this
campaign is where that activity becomes visible.

There is a second, quieter reason. **Community activity is the only campaign material
that generates new knowledge.** A screening day in an underserved area produces
information about that area's health that nobody in the ecosystem had — and that
finding, reported honestly, is worth more than any account of the day itself.

## Business Objectives

- Build standing in the catchment, which is what determines where an ambulance is asked
  to go and where a frightened family drives at 2am.
- Support relationships with local organisations, employers, schools and syndicates —
  the same institutions the ecosystem contracts with commercially.
- Support recruitment and retention: staff want to work somewhere that does this.
- Generate genuine local health knowledge that informs the ecosystem's own planning.
- Strengthen the master brand's claim to be a healthcare platform rather than a hospital
  operator.

## Marketing Objectives

- Make the community the subject of the post, never the institution.
- Report findings rather than activity, wherever findings exist.
- Establish presence in the catchment for people who have never attended.
- Avoid — actively — the reading that this is reputation management.
- Give local partners something they benefit from being part of.

## Positioning

For a community that assumes private hospitals exist to treat people who can pay,
Community Impact shows health work being done where nobody is paying — where competing
campaigns publish photographs of cheques and ceremonies, this one reports what was found
and what it means for the area, with the institution barely in the frame.

> *Test applied:* a competitor can photograph a donation. Publishing what a screening
> day revealed about undiagnosed hypertension in a specific area — and what should be
> done about it — requires having done the work and being willing to make the finding
> useful to everyone, including competitors.

## Brand Philosophy

**Trust before marketing, and the community before the institution.**

`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5 states that trust is built through actions and
experience and is never presented as direct advertising. This campaign is the hardest
test of that rule in the plan, because its content is by nature creditable to the
institution.

The resolution is a single discipline, and it governs every post: **the beneficiary,
the finding or the partner is the subject. The ecosystem is the verb, not the noun.**

## Core Promise

**Everything reported here happened, was done for its own sake, and is reported with
the consent of everyone identifiable in it — and we do not publish what we do for
individuals.**

The promise is about the integrity of the reporting and the limits of it. It makes no
claim about scale or impact.

# ═══════════ HUMAN UNDERSTANDING ═══════════

## Human Problem

What the community is actually living through:

- *"Private hospitals aren't for us."*
- *"They only care once you can pay."*
- *"Nobody comes here. We go to them, or we don't go."*
- *"I've never had my blood pressure checked."*
- *"By the time we go, it's late. That's just how it is."*
- And about the campaign itself: *"They did it for the photo."*

Beneath these: **a large part of the catchment has concluded that the healthcare
system is not addressed to them**, and acts accordingly — presenting late, not
screening, not asking. That conclusion is the actual public health problem in the area,
and it is not corrected by advertising.

## Human Insight

**In this culture, publicising charity is what devalues it — and everyone knows the
rule, including the institution breaking it.**

There is a specific and widely held principle here that giving is worth more when it is
unseen. It is religiously grounded, socially enforced, and universally understood: the
person who announces what they gave has taken payment for it.

Every CSR campaign in this market runs directly into that principle, and audiences
apply it without hesitation. The cheque photograph, the ceremony, the grateful
recipient — each is read as the institution converting a good act into a marketing
asset, and the reading is not cynical. **It is correct.**

> Would a reader think *"how did they know that?"* — yes. Everyone has scrolled past a
> corporate donation photograph and thought less of the donor, and no institution has
> ever acknowledged that the publishing was the problem.

The resolution is not to abandon the campaign. It is to publish **the work, not the
giving** — and specifically to publish the part that is useful to other people:

- What a screening day **found**, not that it happened.
- What a community **needs**, not what was provided.
- What the ecosystem **learned**, and is changing because of it.

A finding is information the reader can use. A donation is a claim about the donor.
Only one of those survives the principle.

The corollary, which is absolute: **individual charity is never published.** A waived
fee, a funded treatment, a family helped — publishing any of it takes payment from
someone at their most vulnerable, and it is the single most damaging thing this
campaign could do.

## Invisible Product

The visible product is an account of community activity.

What the reader is really buying:

- **Being counted.** Evidence that their area is regarded as part of the ecosystem's
  responsibility rather than outside it.
- **Useful information about their own community.** A finding they can act on.
- **A reason to lower the barrier.** The belief that this institution is not only for
  other people.
- **Something to be proud of locally** — a partner organisation, a school, a place that
  took part.

What the ecosystem is really buying is presence: familiarity in the catchment before
there is an emergency.

## Psychological Transformation

**Before:**
> *"That hospital is for people with money. Nothing to do with us, and when they do
> something here it's for their own reasons."*

**After:**
> *"They came, they found something that matters here, and they said what should be
> done about it. They're part of this place."*

The movement is from **outside to part of** — and it is only reachable through
usefulness, never through generosity.

## Emotional Strategy

**Primary emotion: belonging** — the recognition that this place and its people are
regarded as connected.

Supporting: respect, usefulness, quiet pride, trust, gratitude *directed outward*.

Never: self-congratulation, sentimentality, pity, saviour framing, or the register of an
institution being generous. And never gratitude *received* — a grateful beneficiary in
frame is the exact image that converts the work into marketing.

The register is a report, not an announcement. Understated to the point of dryness is
the correct setting.

## Audience

**Primary — the local community in the catchment.**
Including people who have never attended and assume the ecosystem is not for them. They
are the campaign's subject and its most important reader.

**Primary — local partner organisations.**
Schools, employers, syndicates, community bodies and NGOs. They read this campaign to
decide whether working with the ecosystem is worth their time — and their participation
is the campaign's supply of material.

**Secondary — staff.**
People choose employers partly on this, and staff who took part are represented by how
it is reported.

**Secondary — the wider public and physicians.**
Reading the ecosystem's character. Physicians in particular judge CSR harshly and
detect self-promotion instantly.

**Supporting — investors and contract partners.**
Community standing is a commercial asset, but it must never be the visible motivation.

**Who decides, who influences, who pays:** partner organisations decide whether to work
with the ecosystem; local respected figures influence the community's reading; nobody
pays, which is the point.

## Audience Decision

**What they compare:** whether anything actually reached them, and whether the
institution came back. A single event is read as a campaign; a returning presence is
read as commitment.

**What they fear:** being used as material; being pitied; being photographed at a
disadvantage.

**Where they look:** local word of mouth first, always. Social media is secondary in the
catchment and primary for the partner and professional audiences.

**What actually moves them:** repetition and usefulness. Coming back is worth more than
any single event, and a finding they can use is worth more than a service they received
once.

# ═══════════ MEDICAL FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Service Boundaries

- This campaign does **not** publish individual charitable acts — waived fees, funded
  treatments, families helped. Ever.
- It does **not** show identifiable beneficiaries without documented consent, and never
  in a position of need.
- It does **not** show patients, or anyone receiving care, in a way that identifies
  their health status.
- It does **not** state figures — people screened, cases found, money spent — unless the
  operator has supplied and approved them.
- It does **not** claim a health outcome or an impact it cannot evidence.
- It does **not** publish a partner organisation without its agreement.
- It does **not** attach a service promotion, offer or booking ask to community work.
- It does **not** run during a public crisis in a way that uses the crisis.

## Medical Philosophy

Care begins before the visit. `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3 defines continuity of
care as starting before a patient arrives and continuing after they leave; community
activity is the earliest point on that line.

The clinical discipline this campaign requires: **community screening creates clinical
obligations.** Finding a raised blood pressure in a village hall is the beginning of a
duty of care, not the end of an activity. Any post reporting screening must be able to
say what happened to the people whose results were abnormal — and if the answer is
*nothing*, the activity should not have happened and certainly must not be published.

## Core Principles

**The community is the subject.** The institution appears as a participant, never as
the protagonist.

**Publish findings, not generosity.** What was learned, not what was given.

**Never publish individual charity.** The absolute rule of this campaign.

**Consent, from everyone identifiable, freely given.** A person receiving a service
cannot meaningfully refuse to be photographed by the people providing it. The
imbalance is severe and it means consent must be sought separately, afterwards, by
someone with nothing to offer or withhold.

**Never publish what was done in order to be published.** If the activity would not
have happened without the campaign, the campaign should not report it.

**Come back.** A single event is an advertisement; a returning presence is a
relationship. Post about the return more than the first visit.

**Credit outward.** The partner organisation, the school, the volunteers, the local
body — named and thanked. The ecosystem's name appears once, at most.

**No gratitude in frame.** A thankful recipient is the image that converts the whole
campaign into marketing.

## Core Features

The activity territories available to this campaign. Specific activities and findings
must come from the operator; these are the categories that fit the ecosystem's stated
strategy.

**Screening and early detection in the community**
- Blood pressure, blood sugar and basic screening in workplaces, mosques, community
  centres and underserved areas.
- The follow-up pathway for anyone found to need it — the part that makes screening
  legitimate rather than promotional.
- What the screening found about the area, reported as local health information.

**Health education outside the hospital**
- Schools: children, and through them households.
- Workplaces and syndicates: occupational health, and the adults who never attend
  otherwise.
- Community organisations: reaching people who do not read health content.

**Partnership**
- With local NGOs, community bodies, schools, employers and professional syndicates.
- With the ecosystem's role stated accurately: usually the clinical capability, rarely
  the initiative.

**Senior health in the community**
- The natural extension of the Kabarona programme's philosophy into the catchment, and
  the territory where the ecosystem's existing knowledge is deepest.

**Response during public health need**
- Contribution during seasonal or public health pressure, reported factually and
  without using the situation.

**What we learned**
- The most valuable and least used category. Findings from the activity, published as
  information for the area, useful to everyone including other providers.

## Differentiators

**It publishes findings, not donations.** The category norm is the cheque photograph.
Reporting what was learned is a different kind of content entirely, and it is useful to
the reader rather than about the giver.

**It never publishes individual charity.** Which most institutions do, and which every
audience quietly penalises.

**It credits the partner.** Standard practice is for the sponsor to dominate. Naming the
school, the NGO and the volunteers first is unusual enough to be noticed.

**It returns.** Repetition in the same place is the single strongest signal available,
and almost nobody sustains it.

**It carries the ecosystem's follow-up capability.** A screening day run by an
organisation with two hospitals behind it can actually do something about what it finds
— which is the difference between screening and an event, and it is a claim only a
platform can make.

## Service Journey

How community standing is actually built — and therefore the campaign's sequence:

1. **A partner asks, or a need is identified.** Not a marketing plan.
2. **The activity happens**, for its own sake.
3. **Something is found** — about the area's health, or about access.
4. **The people found are followed up.** The obligation created by step 3.
5. **The finding is published**, usefully, with the partner credited.
6. **The ecosystem returns.** The step that converts an event into a relationship.
7. **The finding changes something** internally — a service, a location, a priority —
   and that is the strongest possible post.

Steps 4 and 7 are what separate this from a photocall, and they are where the campaign's
credibility lives.

## What Makes This Different

For a worker reading nothing else: **report what was found and who it matters to, credit
the partner, keep the institution out of the frame, and never publish what was given to
an individual.**

# ═══════════ MARKETING INTELLIGENCE ═══════════

## Communication Objective

To establish presence.

Not to be admired and not to be thanked. The objective is that the community reads the
ecosystem as part of the health of the area — and the test is whether the post would
still be worth publishing if the institution's name were removed from it.

## What We Are Really Selling

**Belonging.**

The visible content is community health activity. What is being sold is a change of
category: from *a private hospital that treats people who can pay* to *part of the
health of this area*.

Restated for a worker: we are not selling generosity, social responsibility or
reputation. We are selling **the fact that this place is included** — and the only proof
of inclusion is having turned up, found something, done something about it, and come
back.

## Human Truth

**Generosity announced is generosity spent.**

The act and its publication are in tension, and every audience knows it. The campaign
survives only by publishing the part that is useful to others rather than the part that
reflects on the giver.

A second truth: **people know who actually shows up.** In a defined catchment, community
presence cannot be faked and does not need to be claimed — the community already knows,
and the campaign's real job is to let everyone else know what the community already
does.

## Audience Psychology

How the audience thinks before anything is said:

- **CSR is public relations.** The default, and usually correct.
- **They did it for the photo.** Applied instantly to any published charity.
- **Private hospitals are not for us.** The belief the campaign exists to change.
- **Announcing what you gave means you wanted something.** The cultural principle, held
  strongly and applied consistently.
- **They will not come back.** Because usually nobody does — which is why returning is
  the most persuasive thing available.

## Emotional Triggers

**Primary:** belonging.

**Supporting:** respect, usefulness, quiet pride, trust.

Deliberately excluded: pity, saviour framing, sentimentality, pride in the institution,
and gratitude directed at the ecosystem. `AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §8 forbids
emotional manipulation, and a grateful beneficiary is the clearest form of it available
in this campaign.

## Psychological Barriers

**Barrier 1 — "This is for the photo."**
The default reading and the campaign's central problem. Corrected by publishing findings
rather than giving, by crediting the partner, and by returning.

**Barrier 2 — "Private hospitals aren't for us."**
The belief that keeps a catchment away. Corrected only by presence, repeated, over time.

**Barrier 3 — "They came once and never again."**
Corrected by making the return the story rather than the launch.

**Barrier 4 — "They're using us."**
Held most strongly by the communities the campaign most wants to reach. Corrected by
consent sought properly, by never showing need, and by the beneficiary being a
participant rather than an exhibit.

**Barrier 5 — "What actually happened to the people they found?"**
The question a sceptical professional reader asks immediately. Corrected by having an
answer — which means the follow-up pathway must exist before the activity does.

## Core Messaging Framework

Consistently reinforced:

- The health of this area is our concern, not only the patients who reach us.
- Here is what we found, and what it means for the people who live here.
- This was done with [partner], and they did most of it.
- We came back.
- Care begins before anyone arrives at a hospital.
- What we learned changed something we do.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. **The community, the place, the partner** — the subject.
2. **What was done**, briefly and factually.
3. **What was found** — the useful part.
4. **What happened next** — follow-up, and what changed.
5. **The ecosystem** — named once, at the end, as a participant.

Never reverse it. A post that opens with the institution is a corporate announcement and
will be read as one within a line.

If there is only one line, it is the finding.

## Narrative Themes

- **What we found.** A screening day reported as local health information.
- **We came back.** The return visit, made the story.
- **The partner did most of it.** Credit directed outward as the post's frame.
- **What it changed.** A finding that altered a service, a location or a priority.
- **The people who never come.** Access as a subject, honestly examined.
- **The school.** Health education for children, and what it reaches at home.
- **The workplace.** Adults who are never screened otherwise.
- **What we did not manage.** An honest account of a limit — the most credible post this
  campaign can publish, and the least likely to be written.
- **Before the hospital.** Community work framed as the earliest point of continuity of
  care.

## Content Pillars

**1. What We Found** — findings as local health information.
**2. Who We Did It With** — partners, credited.
**3. We Came Back** — continuity in the community.
**4. What Changed** — the internal consequence of a finding.
**5. Before The Hospital** — care that begins outside the walls.
**6. What We Have Not Managed** — the honest limits.

## Storytelling Opportunities

Framings, not topics — each with the institution out of the frame:

- A screening table in a community hall, photographed from behind, and the number of
  people who had never had a reading before.
- A partner organisation's coordinator explaining what their community needs.
- The same location, twice, months apart.
- A finding stated plainly as information: what this area's screening revealed.
- A school health session, photographed as a classroom rather than as an event.
- The follow-up call to someone whose reading was high — described, never shown.
- A service or a location changed because of what a community day found.
- An honest post about a community the ecosystem has not reached.

## Communication Style

Always: factual, understated, specific, generous in credit, useful.

Never: congratulatory, sentimental, promotional, pitying, or celebratory.

The register is a report to the community rather than an announcement about the
institution. Dryness is a feature here — warmth reads as performance in this campaign in
a way it does not in any other.

## Tone of Voice

Write as someone reporting back to the area about its own health, who happened to be
part of the work.

Name the partner first. Name the place. Report what was found. Mention the ecosystem
once, plainly, without adjectives.

Never use: *proud to*, *committed to giving back*, *making a difference*, *touched
lives*, *reaching out to the less fortunate*. That is the vocabulary of the category
this campaign must not be mistaken for.

## Messaging Rules

- Always make the community, the partner or the finding the subject.
- Always credit partners and volunteers first and by name, with their agreement.
- Always report what was found where a finding exists.
- Always be able to say what happened to the people who needed follow-up.
- Always obtain consent separately and afterwards, from someone with nothing to give or
  withhold.
- Never publish an individual charitable act.
- Never show a beneficiary in need, in distress, or expressing gratitude.
- Never state a figure the operator has not approved.
- Never attach a service promotion, offer or booking ask.
- Never publish activity that was undertaken in order to be published.
- Never use a public crisis as an opportunity.
- Never let the ecosystem's name appear more than once.

# ═══════════ OFFERS ═══════════

## Current Offers

**No offers.** A promotional offer attached to community work converts the work into
customer acquisition, which is the reading this campaign spends all its effort avoiding.

## Offer Rules

- No promotional offer, price, discount or package may appear in this campaign.
- Free or subsidised community services are **not offers** and must never be framed as
  such. A free screening day is a clinical activity; presenting it as a deal makes it a
  promotion.
- No service may be recommended as a follow-on. Where someone needs care, the post says
  they were referred — not where to book.

## Call To Action

In priority order, and all of them soft:

1. **None.** The default. A report needs no ask.
2. **Take part / get in touch** — for organisations that want to host or partner. The
   only CTA that grows the campaign's supply of real activity.
3. **Act on the finding** — where the finding implies something a reader can do, stated
   generally and without naming a service.
4. **Follow the page** — weakest, and rarely appropriate here.

Never a booking CTA. Never a donation ask — the ecosystem is not a charity and
collecting from the public would change what this campaign is.

## Primary KPI

**Local reach and partner enquiries.**

Reach specifically within the catchment, not general reach — a community campaign seen
nationally and not locally has failed at its only purpose. The stronger measure is
whether local organisations approach the ecosystem to partner, because that indicates
the campaign is read as genuine.

Sentiment is the third measure, and the one to watch: any drift toward *"they did it for
the photo"* means a post crossed the line, and the line should be re-read rather than
the post defended.

# ═══════════ STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE ═══════════

## Strategic Reminder

Before publishing, ask the test question: **would this post still be worth publishing if
our name were removed from it?**

If yes, it is a community post. If no, it is an advertisement about generosity, and it
will be read as one.

And ask the second: **did we do this because it needed doing, or because it would be
published?** If the second, it does not run — and the activity should be reconsidered.

## Strategic Constraints

- Never publish individual charity. Absolute.
- Never use a beneficiary's need or gratitude as material.
- Never publish an activity undertaken for the publication.
- Never compete on social responsibility, or compare with other institutions.
- Never let community work stand in for the ecosystem's obligations to its own patients.
- Consent outranks every campaign consideration and is revocable without discussion.
- Long-term brand value outranks short-term engagement
  (`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §20.5).

## Medical Communication Principles

- Community screening creates a duty of care. No screening activity may be published
  unless the follow-up pathway existed and was used.
- No individual's health status may be identifiable, in image or in text.
- No clinical advice is given through this campaign.
- Findings are reported as observations from an activity, never as epidemiological
  claims about a population.
- Nothing may imply that community activity substitutes for medical care.

## Never Promise

The campaign must never promise:

- Continued or recurring community activity in a given place.
- Free or subsidised care to any individual or group.
- Any health outcome or impact from the activity.
- Any figure — people reached, cases found, spend — not approved by the operator.
- That findings generalise beyond the people actually seen.
- Follow-up care to anyone who was not already offered it.
- Partnership with an organisation that has not agreed.

## Can Promise

The campaign can confidently state:

- That the activity described happened, with the partner named.
- That care in this ecosystem is understood to begin before a hospital visit
  (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3).
- That education precedes promotion as a stated principle (§5).
- That people identified as needing follow-up were referred into a pathway that exists.
- That everyone identifiable consented.
- That what was found is being used internally.

## Brand Perception

After sustained exposure, the community should believe:

**"They actually come here, and they tell us useful things. They're part of this place —
not a hospital across town that wants our money."**

Partners should believe: *"Working with them is worth our time, and they will not use us
for a photograph."*

## Long-Term Brand Contribution

This campaign builds standing in the catchment, which is the asset that decides the
decisions made with no time to think — where an ambulance goes, where a family drives at
2am, which hospital a local employer contracts with.

It is also the ecosystem's most credible evidence for its central claim. INSAN positions
itself as a platform developing healthcare rather than a business treating patients;
work done where nobody is paying is the only unambiguous proof of that distinction.

And it compounds only through repetition. A single year of community activity produces
almost nothing; five years of returning to the same places produces a position no
competitor can enter.

## Relationship With INSAN

**Trust platform: Continuity.**

Community work is the earliest point in continuity of care — before the visit, before
the symptom, before the patient is a patient. That is a platform claim rather than a
hospital one: an individual hospital treats its catchment as a market, while a platform
that manages institutions can treat a population as its subject.

It is also where the ecosystem's scale is genuinely useful rather than merely stated: a
screening day backed by two hospitals can follow up what it finds. That is the
difference between community health work and a community event.

Per `AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §16, this campaign runs most naturally on the INSAN
page, with hospital pages carrying activity in their own catchment. Cross-promotion must
be especially light — a branded frame around a community finding is exactly the intrusion
this campaign is built to avoid.

# ═══════════ INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE ═══════════

## Institutional Knowledge Extensions

**The cultural principle is not a nuance; it is the campaign's governing constraint.**
Giving unseen is understood here as worth more than giving announced, and the principle
is applied to institutions as readily as to people. Every decision in this file follows
from it: findings instead of donations, partners credited first, the institution named
once, and individual charity never published at all. A writer who does not understand
this will produce a post that performs adequately and costs the ecosystem more than it
gains.

**Individual charity must be invisible, and this needs saying inside the organisation
too.** The ecosystem will help individual families — waive a fee, fund a treatment,
absorb a cost. It should. None of it is ever published, and staff who are proud of it
need to know why the rule exists rather than experiencing it as modesty. Publishing it
takes payment from a person at their most vulnerable, and it makes the next family
wonder whether accepting help means being used.

**Screening without follow-up is worse than no screening.** Finding raised blood
pressure in a community hall and leaving is an activity that generated anxiety and
provided nothing. The pathway must exist before the day happens. This is the operational
precondition of the campaign, not a communication preference — and it is the thing a
sceptical physician reader will check first.

**The catchment is the boundary.** Community reach outside the area the hospitals serve
is not community impact; it is publicity. The campaign's measure is local reach for that
reason.

**What this file cannot supply.** Which activities the ecosystem actually runs, which
partners exist, what has been found, whether follow-up pathways are in place, and the
geography of the catchment itself. All of it is operator knowledge, and none of this
campaign can run without it — this file supplies the discipline, not the material.
