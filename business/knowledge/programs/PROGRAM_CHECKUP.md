# Check-up Programs (برامج الفحص الشامل)

## 1. Foundation

### 1.1 Overview

Four packaged health-assessment programmes, each completed **in a single visit**,
escalating from a basic screen to a full multi-specialty assessment. They run at
**both Future and Delta**.

Every programme combines consultations, laboratory work, imaging and a period of
hosted stay, and ends with a written report and a plan.

### 1.2 Definition

**⚠️ CAPACITY: a maximum of five cases per week.** *(Brand owner, 2026-08-08.)*
This is not a detail — it governs how the programme may be marketed and what the
receptionist may promise. See §4.3.

### 1.3 Vision ✍️

To make an annual check-up an ordinary thing an Egyptian family does — so that
disease is found while it is still cheap, treatable and quiet, instead of at the
emergency door.

*Derived from §1.1 (assessment before symptoms) and the Basic programme's own
line, «اطمئن على صحتك قبل ظهور المرض».*

### 1.4 Why This Service Exists

Because the conditions these programmes look for — diabetes, hypertension,
cardiac risk, thyroid disorder, osteoporosis, occult bleeding — are **silent
until they are not.** A person over 55 can feel entirely well and be years into
one of them.

And because getting the same picture piecemeal means a dozen appointments across
a dozen places over months, which most people abandon partway. These programmes
compress it into **one visit, one building, one report**.

1.  **Make a check-up feel normal rather than alarming.** The audience is not

### 1.7 Positioning

**The whole picture of your health, in one day, from consultants — not a lab
receipt.**

The distinction that matters: a laboratory sells tests and hands back numbers.
These programmes are **consultants reading those numbers together in the same
building on the same day**, and ending with one plan rather than seven results.

### 1.8 Brand Philosophy

*«أساس الخدمة الطبية احترام الإنسان»* — respect for the human being is the
foundation of medical service. Here that means a check-up **designed around an
older person's day**: a hosted stay rather than a corridor, a meal, rest between
examinations, and in the higher tiers a coordinator who walks with them.

### 1.9 Core Promise ✍️

**In one day you will know where your health actually stands — and you will
leave with a written plan, not a folder of numbers to interpret alone.**

Bounded by §7.2: a check-up cannot promise that nothing will be found, nor that
everything will be.

*Derived from §1.7 Positioning and the deliverables every programme lists —

## 2. Human Understanding

### 2.1 Human Problem

The person this is for **feels fine.** There is no pain sending them, no symptom
forcing a decision, and nothing on any particular day that makes it today rather
than next year.

Meanwhile they carry a low, constant unease about their age and their family
history — and a sharper fear underneath it: that a check-up will find something.

### 2.2 Human Insight ✍️

**This is the only service in the ecosystem whose patient is not in trouble** —
and that changes everything about how it is sold.

Everywhere else there is a symptom doing the persuading: pain, a lump, a child's
fever. Here there is nothing. The person is well, busy, and being asked to spend
a day and money on a problem they do not have.

And the resistance is not laziness. **It is that they do not want to be told.**
As long as no one has looked, they are healthy. A check-up converts a comfortable
uncertainty into a fact they will then have to live with — and at 60, with
children and a mortgage and a business, that is a real thing to avoid.

So the barrier is not "is this hospital good", and it is not price. **It is that
finding out feels like losing something.** Content that shouts about equipment
argues with a decision nobody has made yet.

*Derived from §2.1 Human Problem and the Basic programme's framing — «اطمئن على
صحتك قبل ظهور المرض» — which addresses a well person, not a patient.*

### 2.3 Invisible Product

Not tests. **Permission to stop wondering.** And, for the family around them,
evidence that the person they worry about has been properly looked at by someone
qualified.

### 2.4 Psychological Transformation

*   **Before:** "I'm fine, God willing." Vague unease, no plan, and quietly not
    wanting to know.
*   **After:** Knows the actual numbers, has a written plan for the year, and has
    stopped guessing.

### 2.5 Emotional Strategy ✍️

**Reframe from detection to reassurance, and never sell fear.**

The programme's own name does this already — *الوقاية والاطمئنان*, prevention and
peace of mind. Most people who take a check-up are fine, and **saying so is the
honest and the more persuasive message**: most results come back reassuring, and
the ones that do not are found early enough to matter.

⚠️ **Fear-based content is forbidden here** and the reason is specific: §2.2 says
the audience is already avoiding the subject. Raising the alarm gives an
avoidant person a stronger reason to avoid.

The second lever is **the family**. §2.7 makes adult children a real
decision-maker, and "اطمن على والدك" reaches a person who is not resisting at
all.

*Derived from §2.2 Human Insight, §2.7 Audience Decision and the programmes' own
naming.*

### 2.6 Audience

*   **Adults over 55** — the Basic programme states the age explicitly.
*   **People with cardiac or diabetic risk**, or a family history of it — the
    Advanced programme's whole design.
*   **Adult children of older parents**, who often initiate and pay.
*   **Executives and business owners** for whom a half-day is the constraint —
    the VIP 360 tier.

### 2.7 Audience Decision ✍️

Frequently **not made by the patient.** An adult son or daughter books it,
gives it as a gift, or insists. The older person consents rather than chooses.

That has a direct consequence: **two different people must be convinced by the
same post.** The son needs to believe it is thorough and worth the money; the
father needs to believe it will not be exhausting or frightening. The hosted
stay, the meal, the rest between examinations and the coordinator answer the
second person, and they are usually written as luxury when they are actually
**reassurance**.

*Derived from §2.6 Audience and the programmes' stay arrangements.*

## 3. Medical Foundation

### 3.1 Service Boundaries

*   **Included:** consultations, laboratory work, imaging, the hosted stay, the
    report, the electronic medical file, and the plan.
*   **Excluded:** treatment of anything found. The check-up produces a plan; the
    treatment is a separate episode, in the relevant centre.

### 3.2 Medical Philosophy

Assessment before symptoms, and **the whole picture read together**. Several
items are marked *عند الحاجة* — bone density, PSA, prostate and gynaecological
ultrasound — which means a consultant decides, not the package. That is
deliberate and it is a claim worth making: **the programme does not run every
test on every person to justify a price.**

### 3.3 Core Principles

One visit · consultants rather than technicians · a written plan rather than
loose results · a stay designed for an older person · escalation by need, not by
default.

### 3.4 Core Features

*   **Four tiers**, from a two-consultant basic screen to a seven-consultant
    full assessment.
*   **Single-visit completion** — 3–4 hours at Basic, up to a half day at VIP.
*   **A dedicated ward or suite**, with a meal and rest between examinations.
*   **A medical coordinator** accompanying the patient throughout, from the
    Golden tier.
*   **An electronic medical file**, so the visit becomes a baseline rather than a
    one-off.
*   **A plan on leaving** — up to a full year's treatment plan and a nutrition
    programme at Golden.
*   **Sex-specific pathways** — urology for men, obstetrics & gynaecology for
    women, from the Golden tier.

### 3.5 Differentiators ✍️

*   **Consultants, not a laboratory.** Every tier begins with people, and the
    tests exist to inform them.
*   **It ends with a plan.** A check-up that hands back numbers has moved the
    work to the patient. All four tiers end with a report and a plan; Golden adds
    a year-long treatment plan and a diet programme.
*   **Designed for an older body.** A seated, hosted, fed, paced day — not a
    corridor and a queue.
*   **Escalation by need.** Several items are explicitly *عند الحاجة*.
*   **Both hospitals.**

*Derived from §3.2 Medical Philosophy and §3.4 Core Features.*

### 3.6 Service Journey

Booking → arrival at a dedicated reception → consultations → laboratory and
imaging, with rest and a meal between → the consultants' assessment → report,
electronic file and plan on leaving → **follow-up after the results are
received**, which the Basic programme states explicitly.

### 3.7 What Makes This Different

That it **finishes**. The comparison is not another hospital's check-up — it is
the version most people actually attempt, which is a test here and an appointment
there over three months, abandoned halfway with no one having looked at the whole
picture.

### 3.8 Clinic Schedule

Bookings are arranged individually. **Capacity is five cases per week** (§1.2),
so this is not a standing clinic session.

⚠️ Day-to-day outpatient clinic times are in
`business/knowledge/hospitals/CLINIC_SCHEDULES.md`.

### 4.3 ⚠️ Capacity — five cases a week

**The brand owner set this on 2026-08-08: a maximum of five cases per week.**

**Why it belongs at the front of this file rather than in a footnote:** it is a
hard limit on a service being advertised, and it constrains marketing at least as
much as it constrains operations. Roughly **twenty cases a month**, and each one
occupies several consultants for most of a day.

**What follows from it:**

*   **The campaign builds intent, not urgency.** A post that generates a hundred
    enquiries in a day has generated ninety-five disappointments and a hundred
    people who now associate the hospital with being turned away.
*   **Never advertise immediate availability.** No "احجز النهاردة", no
    "أماكن محدودة" used as a pressure device — the scarcity is real, and using it
    to rush people is both manipulative and pointless when the queue is already
    full.
*   **The waiting list is the product, not the failure.** Being booked out is
    consistent with a serious, consultant-heavy assessment.

## 5. Marketing Intelligence

### 5.1 Communication Objective

To move a well person from "لو تعبت هروح" to "أنا بكشف كل سنة" — a change of
habit, not a change of supplier.

### 5.2 What We Are Really Selling ✍️

**Certainty, and a plan.** Not tests, and not equipment.

For the adult child paying, something else entirely: **the relief of having done
something** about a parent they worry about and cannot otherwise help.

*Derived from §2.3 Invisible Product and §2.7 Audience Decision.*

### 5.3 Human Truth

People do not avoid check-ups because they doubt the medicine. They avoid them
because they would rather not know — and because nothing today forces the issue.

### 5.4 Audience Psychology ✍️

**The well person over 55.** Not comparing hospitals; not thinking about it at
all. Postpones without ever refusing. Moved by a specific occasion — a birthday,
a friend's diagnosis, a grandchild — far more than by argument.

**The adult child.** Actively looking, comparing, and carrying guilt. The most
reachable audience the programme has, and the one that converts. Wants
thoroughness and a name they trust.

**The person with a known risk** — diabetic, hypertensive, cardiac family
history. Already half-convinced and needs the practical answer: what is included,
how long, what happens after.

**The executive.** Constrained by time, not money. The half-day VIP tier is
written for this person.

*Derived from §2.6, §2.7, §2.1 and the four programmes' own design.*

### 5.5 Emotional Triggers

The birthday that ends in a five or a zero. A friend of the same age
hospitalised. A grandchild. A parent who "لسه بيقول أنا كويس" while the family
worries.

### 5.6 Psychological Barriers

*   **"I'm fine, why would I go?"** — no symptom, no trigger.
*   **The fear of being told** (§2.2) — the real barrier and the least spoken.
*   **Cost, for a service producing no immediate relief.**
*   **A day is a lot** for someone who feels well.
*   **"They'll find something to sell me."** Answered directly by §3.2's
    *عند الحاجة* items.

### 5.7 Core Messaging Framework

> «اطمئن على صحتك قبل ظهور المرض.»
> «صورة كاملة لصحتك في يوم واحد — مع استشاريين، وبخطة تمشي عليها.»

### 5.8 Narrative Themes ✍️

*   **Silent for years.** What diabetes, hypertension and osteoporosis do before
    anyone notices.
*   **Most people come out fine** — the reassurance frame that answers §2.2
    instead of arguing with it.
*   **A day, not three months.** Against the piecemeal version people abandon
    (§3.7).
*   **What "عند الحاجة" means.** The centre declining to run a test — the direct
    answer to "they'll find something to sell me".
*   **اطمن على والدك.** The programme told from the adult child's side (§2.7).
*   **The plan, not the results.** What the patient leaves holding.

*Derived from §2.2, §3.2, §3.7, §5.6 and §2.7.*

### 5.9 Content Pillars ✍️

1.  **Numbers That Matter After 55** — HbA1c, lipids, kidney and liver function,
    bone density: what each one is and what it warns of. Education, not selling.
2.  **What Is In Each Programme** — one per tier, plainly, so the reader can
    self-select.
3.  **Silent Conditions** — the anchor pillar. What is found early versus what is
    found late.
4.  **For Your Parents** — written to the adult child (§2.7).
5.  **The Day Itself** — arrival, consultants, rest, the meal, the report. Answers
    "a day is a lot" by showing it is a comfortable one.

*Derived from §5.8, §2.7 and §1.2's programme contents.*

### 5.10 Storytelling Opportunities ✍️

*   The consultant handing over the report and walking through the plan.
*   The dedicated ward — armchair, meal, no queue.
*   The coordinator walking beside a patient between departments (Golden, VIP).
*   The moment a result is explained in plain language and the patient's face

*   An adult child booking it for a parent.
*   The DEXA scan or the echo, filmed as unthreatening.

⚠️ **No identifiable patient with a finding.** Nobody's diagnosis appears with

*Derived from §3.4, §3.6 and §5.5.*

### 5.11 Communication Style

Warm, calm, educational. Explains a number without frightening anyone about it.

### 5.12 Tone of Voice

An unhurried consultant explaining to someone's parent — respectful of age,
never patronising.

### 5.13 Messaging Rules

*   **Never fear-based** (§2.5).
*   **Never state a price** — none is recorded.
*   **Never promise a result**, in either direction.
*   **Never advertise immediate availability** (§4.3).

*   No price exists anywhere.

### 7.2 Never Promise

*   **Never that the results will be reassuring.** A check-up finds what it
    finds, and a campaign that implies a clean result has promised something no
    examination can.
*   **Never that a condition will be caught.** Screening reduces risk; it does
    not eliminate it.
*   **Never a specific appointment date** before the hospital confirms.
*   **Never a price** before booking.
*   **Never that every test runs for every patient** — several are *عند الحاجة*.

### 7.3 Can Promise ✍️

*   The consultants, tests, imaging and stay listed for each programme in §1.2 —
    programme four's imaging excepted.
*   **Completion in a single visit**, within the stated hours.
*   **A written report, an electronic medical file and a plan on leaving.**
*   **Follow-up after the results are received** (Basic states it; treat it as
    the standard).
*   A consultant deciding the *عند الحاجة* items, rather than the package.
*   Availability at **both hospitals**.

*Derived from §7.2's boundary and the programme contents in §1.2.*

### 7.4 Brand Perception ✍️

**The place that looks at the whole person in a day and tells you the truth about
what it found.**

Not "the cheapest check-up" and not "the most tests". The perception worth owning
is **thoroughness with restraint** — every consultant needed and no test that is
not, which is what §3.2's *عند الحاجة* items demonstrate.

*Derived from §1.7 Positioning, §3.2 and §5.6.*

### 7.5 Relationship With INSAN ✍️

**Trust platform: Transparency**, supported by **Continuity**.

The platform's standard is *«أساس الخدمة الطبية احترام الإنسان»*. A check-up is
the most commercially tempting service a hospital runs — the patient is well,
compliant, and has no way to judge whether a test was needed. **The respect is in
what is not run.**

The checkable behaviours:

*   Marking tests *عند الحاجة* and letting a consultant decide (§3.2).
*   Ending with a plan the patient understands, not a folder of numbers.
*   Pacing the day around an older person — a seat, a meal, rest, a coordinator.
*   Never implying a result in advance (§7.2).
*   Never using the real capacity limit as a pressure device (§4.3).
*   Following up after the results arrive, so the visit is a beginning.

**Continuity** because the electronic medical file makes this year's numbers next
year's baseline — which is the difference between a check-up and a relationship.
