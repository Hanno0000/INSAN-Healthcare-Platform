# Purpose

This document is the Source of Truth for the **Why Trust Us** campaign.

It describes a campaign, not a medical entity. Where a medical service file answers
*what is this department*, this file answers *what is this campaign, what belongs
inside it, and what must never appear in it.*

Three scheduled posts depend on it.

# Document Philosophy

Written for a worker that has never seen INSAN and cannot ask. Everything here is
derived from `MASTER_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`,
`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md`, `PROJECT_DECISIONS.md` and the Organisational Trust
Model in `departments/MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`.

**This is the most dangerous campaign in the ecosystem to write badly.** Its subject is
trustworthiness, and asserting trustworthiness is the single most reliable way to
undermine it. Read **Human Insight** before writing a word.

# ═══════════ FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Overview

Why Trust Us is the campaign that answers the question every other campaign provokes:
**why should I believe any of this?**

It supplies evidence rather than assurance — governance, transparency, the limits the
ecosystem places on itself, and the mechanisms by which its claims can be checked. It
is the campaign that exists because everything else in the plan is a claim.

## Definition

A corporate campaign that makes the ecosystem's trustworthiness **checkable**, by
showing the mechanisms that produce it and the limits that constrain it.

**What is inside it:** governance and audit; transparency as an operating practice —
clinical, financial and process; what the ecosystem refuses to promise and why; how
standards are enforced rather than declared; how complaints and failures are handled;
the consistency of behaviour across both hospitals; the ethical constraints the
ecosystem publishes about itself.

**What is outside it:**

- **What INSAN is** — the model, the structure. That is *Why INSAN?*, and it comes
  first.
- **What INSAN believes** — values and philosophy. That is *Brand Identity*.
- **What INSAN thinks healthcare should be** — sector argument. That is *Healthcare
  Leadership*.
- **Delta's specific trust position.** Delta carries its own restored-trust campaign
  at the hospital level. This campaign is about the ecosystem's trustworthiness in
  general and must not be used to address one hospital's local reputation.
- **Process documentation.** Showing a sterilisation cycle is *Behind The Scenes*.
  This campaign may reference that such campaigns exist; it does not do their work.
- **Patient testimony.** That is *Success Stories* and *Patient Journey*.
- Any claim of superiority over another institution.

### The corporate routing test

| The question the reader is asking | The campaign |
|---|---|
| "What is this?" | Why INSAN? |
| "What do you believe?" | Brand Identity |
| **"Why should I believe you?"** | **Why Trust Us** |
| "What do you think healthcare should be?" | Healthcare Leadership |
| "What are you building?" | Healthcare Innovation |
| "Where are you going, and what has changed?" | Growth & Transformation |
| "What does the digital side look like?" | Digital Healthcare Transformation |

**Sequence matters here more than anywhere.** This campaign assumes the reader knows
what INSAN is. Scheduled before *Why INSAN?*, it asks someone to trust a thing they
cannot name — which is the exact behaviour it is trying to argue against.

## Vision

That the ecosystem becomes an institution people trust for reasons they can articulate
— not because it feels reliable, but because they have seen how it constrains itself.

## Mission

Replace assurance with evidence, and make the evidence small enough to be believed.

## Why This Service Exists

**Every claim the ecosystem makes creates a debt, and this campaign is where it is
paid.**

The plan schedules campaigns asserting unified quality, organised care, transparency,
human-centred experience and continuous improvement. Each is a promise. An audience
that has heard identical promises from every institution in this market discounts all
of them automatically — and the discount is not irrational, it is learned.

So there are two options. Assert harder, which increases the discount. Or show the
mechanism, which is the only thing that has ever moved it.

`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` records the conclusion as permanent: **trust is built through
systems rather than advertising**, and institutional trust is created by consistent
organisational behaviour, not by campaigns. That creates an obvious problem for a
campaign about trust — and the resolution is the whole design of this one. It does not
try to create trust. **It documents the mechanisms that create it**, which is
something a campaign can honestly do.

There is a second reason, and it is commercial.
`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §10 weights investors at 50% of brand-building priority.
`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` records that insurers, syndicates and corporate clients
evaluate operational transparency directly. For that audience this is not a reassurance
campaign — it is a due-diligence document in public form.

## Business Objectives

- Convert the ecosystem's claims from assertions into checkable statements.
- Support contract negotiation with insurers, syndicates and corporate clients, who
  evaluate governance directly.
- Support investor confidence through demonstrated operational transparency.
- Reduce the trust cost carried by a young platform and a transforming hospital.
- Establish transparency as an operating characteristic, so that later difficult
  communication is received in an existing frame.

## Marketing Objectives

- Give the audience reasons rather than reassurance.
- Make the ecosystem's self-imposed limits visible — what it will not claim is more
  persuasive than what it will.
- Establish that standards here are enforced, not declared.
- Build the frame in which every other campaign's claims are received.
- Reach the commercial audience with material the consumer audience also respects.

## Positioning

For anyone who has heard every hospital in the market promise quality and has stopped
believing all of them, Why Trust Us shows the mechanisms and the limits instead of
making another promise — where competing campaigns assert excellence, this one
publishes what the ecosystem refuses to claim, and lets that refusal carry the weight.

> *Test applied:* any competitor can say it is trustworthy. Publishing a list of things
> you will never promise is only available to an organisation prepared to be held to
> the list.

## Brand Philosophy

**Trust before marketing.**

`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §5 states it as a strategic differentiator: trust is
built by actions and experience, integrated into content, and never presented as
direct advertising. This campaign is the closest the ecosystem comes to breaking that
rule, and the way it stays inside it is by never claiming trust — only showing the
machinery.

`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §21 forbids unproven claims and forbids saying "the best
hospital" without evidence. Read positively, that ban is this campaign's entire brief:
**say only what can be evidenced, and say the limits out loud.**

## Core Promise

**We will not tell you anything we cannot show you, and we will tell you what we
cannot promise.**

This is a promise about communication rather than about outcomes, which is why it is
keepable. It is also the only kind of trust promise that does not immediately
undermine itself.

# ═══════════ HUMAN UNDERSTANDING ═══════════

## Human Problem

What the audience is actually living through:

- *"Everyone says they're the best. It means nothing."*
- *"They all have the same photos and the same words."*
- *"I've been let down by a hospital before. Why would this be different?"*
- *"They only tell you the good things. Nobody admits anything."*
- *"If something goes wrong, they'll close ranks."*
- *"I can't check any of this. I just have to hope."*
- For a physician: *"Standards on paper, or standards in practice? I've seen both."*
- For an insurer or investor: *"Show me governance, not adjectives."*

Beneath these: **the audience has been asked to trust institutions many times and has
learned that the asking is not correlated with the deserving.** They have no instrument
for telling a good institution from a well-marketed one, and they resent being asked to
try.

## Human Insight

**Claiming to be trustworthy is the strongest available signal that you are not.**

Institutions that deserve trust rarely assert it; they behave in ways that make the
question unnecessary. Everyone knows this intuitively, which is why "why trust us" is
the single most dangerous phrase this ecosystem could put in front of an audience — and
why the campaign named after it must never actually say it.

What does work is **costly honesty**: telling people something true that it would have
been easier not to tell them. A list of things the ecosystem will never promise. An
admission that a hospital cannot guarantee a bed. A statement that the standard is a
process, not an outcome. Each of those costs something — they surrender a marketing
claim — and cost is what makes a signal credible.

> Would a reader think *"how did they know that?"* — yes. Everyone has noticed that the
> loudest claims of trustworthiness come from the least trustworthy places, and no
> healthcare brand has ever acknowledged it while asking for trust.

The strategic form of the insight, and the campaign's operating rule: **the most
persuasive sentence available to this ecosystem is a limit, not a strength.**

## Invisible Product

The visible product is evidence of governance.

What the reader is really buying:

- **Permission to stop being vigilant.** Suspicion is exhausting; they would rather
  put it down and need a reason.
- **A basis for a decision they can defend** to a spouse, a parent or a board.
- **Predictability of behaviour** — knowing how the institution will act if something
  goes wrong, before it does.
- **Freedom from the marketing relationship.** Being spoken to as an adult rather than
  a prospect.
- For the commercial audience: **de-risked partnership.** Governance is what makes an
  institution contractible.

The deepest one: **the belief that they will not be lied to.** Everything else in the
campaign is instrumental to that.

## Psychological Transformation

**Before:**
> *"They all say the same things. I'll go and hope, and I'll find out afterwards
> whether they meant it."*

**After:**
> *"They told me what they won't promise. That's the first honest thing I've read from
> a hospital. If they're straight about the limits, they're probably straight about the
> rest."*

The movement is from **discounting everything** to **having a reason to weigh one
institution differently** — which is all any trust campaign can honestly achieve.

## Emotional Strategy

**Primary emotion: reassurance**, of the kind produced by candour rather than comfort.

Supporting: respect, relief, confidence, being taken seriously.

Never: defensiveness, indignation, moral superiority, or the wounded tone of an
institution insisting on its own integrity. And never fear — this campaign must not
imply that the alternative is dangerous, which `AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §21
forbids and which would in any case make the reader suspect a sales motive.

The register is plain and slightly austere. Warmth here reads as persuasion; flatness
reads as honesty.

## Audience

Per `AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §10, corporate brand-building weights investors 50%,
doctors 30%, public 20%.

**Primary — insurers, syndicates, corporate clients and investors.**
`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` records that this audience evaluates operational transparency,
occupancy clarity and measurable performance. They read this campaign as evidence, and
they are the audience most able to act on it.

**Primary — physicians, referring and prospective.**
The most sceptical readers the ecosystem has. They have worked inside institutions that
had excellent written standards and did not follow them, and they can tell the
difference in three sentences.

**Secondary — patients and families with a decision to make.**
Especially anyone choosing where to have surgery or where to take a critically ill
relative. They cannot evaluate governance but they can recognise candour.

**Secondary — patients who have been let down before.**
A large and quiet audience. They are not weighing this hospital against another; they
are weighing whether to believe any hospital.

**Supporting — staff.**
What the institution publishes about its own standards becomes what staff are held to,
and what they expect of it.

**Who decides, who influences, who pays:** for the commercial audience, a committee
decides on documented governance; for patients, the family member who has been let down
before influences most.

## Audience Decision

**What they compare:** the commercial audience compares governance documents and
operational data; physicians compare how an institution behaves when something goes
wrong; patients compare feelings and reputation, because nothing better is available to
them.

**What they fear:** being lied to; discovering after the fact that the standard was on
paper only; an institution that closes ranks; being blamed for their own outcome.

**Where they look:** the commercial audience asks directly and reads documents;
physicians ask colleagues who have worked there; patients ask relatives and read
comments.

**What actually moves them:** a specific self-imposed limit. *"We cannot guarantee a
bed, and we will tell you honestly when we do not have one"* moves every one of these
audiences simultaneously, because it is a statement no marketing department would write
unless it were true.

# ═══════════ MEDICAL FOUNDATION ═══════════

## Service Boundaries

- This campaign does **not** claim any clinical outcome, success rate or safety record.
- It does **not** claim accreditation, certification, ranking or award the ecosystem
  does not hold.
- It does **not** publish figures — infection rates, complaint volumes, audit scores —
  unless the operator has supplied and approved them.
- It does **not** compare the ecosystem to another institution, explicitly or by
  implication.
- It does **not** address any specific past incident, complaint or reputational event.
- It does **not** carry Delta's restored-trust story. That is a hospital campaign.
- It does **not** use patient testimony as proof.
- It does **not** discuss litigation, regulation or any dispute.

## Medical Philosophy

Trust in medicine is produced by process, not by outcome. A patient can receive
excellent care and a poor result; the institution's integrity is in how the care was
organised, communicated and accounted for — not in whether the illness cooperated.

`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md` states the consequence directly: the ICU does not promise
medical outcomes; it promises organised care, communication, collaboration and
transparency. This campaign extends that discipline to the whole ecosystem, and it is
what allows the campaign to speak about trust without making a medical claim.

## Core Principles

**Never say "trust us".** The campaign's own name must never appear as an ask. Show the
mechanism and let the reader conclude.

**Lead with a limit.** The most persuasive material available is what the ecosystem
refuses to claim.

**One mechanism per post.** Governance is abstract; a single, specific mechanism is
not. Three vague assurances are worth less than one described process.

**Never publish an unverified figure.** In a campaign about honesty, an unsupported
number is not a small error — it is a self-refuting one.

**Nothing defensive.** The campaign never responds, never argues, and never implies an
accusation exists. Defensiveness introduces a doubt the reader did not have.

**Say the same thing to everyone.** A claim made to insurers and not to patients — or
the reverse — is the failure mode this campaign exists to prevent.

**Behaviour outranks the post.** If the institution does not do the thing described, the
post is a lie with a long tail. Verify before publishing, always.

## Core Features

The evidence available to this campaign. All of it is recorded in the committed
documents, and none of it requires an operational figure.

**Governance and standards** — `MASTER_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`
- INSAN sets standards, governance frameworks and quality systems centrally.
- The standards are trained, applied and audited rather than published and left.
- Operational review is continuous, not periodic.
- The same standard applies at both hospitals — a claim that can be shown side by side.

**Transparency as an operating value** — `PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §3, §7
- Radical transparency: clarity about procedures, costs and available alternatives.
- Clinical communication transparency: families understand what is happening and why.
- Financial transparency: honest discussion of expected cost from the beginning, with
  guidance on payment pathways and no hidden fees.
- Process transparency: an explained admission process and honest communication about
  capacity.

**Self-imposed limits** — `MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`, *What The ICU Should Never Promise*
- No guaranteed survival, recovery or prevention of complications.
- No guaranteed admission, bed availability or timing.
- Nothing beyond ethical medical responsibility.
- Published as commitments rather than as disclaimers, this is the campaign's single
  strongest asset.

**What can be promised, and is** — *What The ICU Can Confidently Promise*
- Organised care, professional multidisciplinary management, honest communication,
  respectful interaction, physician collaboration, transparent process, ethical
  decision-making, continuous monitoring.
- Every item describes a process, which is why every item is keepable.

**Ethical constraints the ecosystem publishes about itself**
- Admission is decided on medical criteria, never on occupancy targets or ability to
  pay (`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`, *Ethical ICU Utilization*).
- A declined admission is handled with honesty, explanation and alternative guidance.
- Marketing constraints: no fear, no clickbait, no discounts as a primary message, no
  unproven claims (`AI_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md` §21). The ecosystem publishes the
  rules it holds its own communication to, which is itself a trust mechanism.

**Accountability** — `MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`, *Professional Accountability*
- The system holds itself accountable for quality of care, communication timeliness,
  financial honesty, operational efficiency and patient experience.
- Accountability is built in rather than imposed externally.

**The Trust Equation** — recorded as permanent
> Trust = Transparency + Organisation + Communication + Teamwork + Professionalism +
> Consistency

No single element is sufficient. This is the campaign's structural backbone: six
elements, each of which can carry its own post.

## Differentiators

**It publishes limits.** Almost no institution in this market states what it will not
promise. Doing so is unusual enough to be noticed and costly enough to be believed.

**Its trust model is written down and permanent.** The Trust Equation and the
Organisational Trust Model are recorded strategic conclusions, not campaign lines —
which means the campaign is describing something the organisation actually operates on.

**It refuses the easiest available move.** Every competitor's trust campaign uses
patient testimony. This one does not, because testimony is evidence of an experience
and not of a system — and the ecosystem's claim is systemic.

**It says the same thing to patients and to insurers.** Consistency across audiences is
rare, checkable, and exactly what a governance-led claim requires.

**Consistency across two hospitals is demonstrable.** The same standard, visible in two
buildings, is a form of proof unavailable to a single institution.

## Service Journey

How trust is actually formed, in order — and therefore the sequence available to this
campaign:

1. **Prior disappointment.** The reader arrives already discounted.
2. **A surprising limit.** Something the institution declines to claim.
3. **Re-reading.** The reader looks again, because that was unexpected.
4. **A mechanism.** How a standard is actually enforced.
5. **Consistency.** The same thing said elsewhere, to a different audience.
6. **A test.** Contact with the institution — a phone call, a visit — that matches.
7. **Provisional trust.** Extended, and monitored.

Step 2 is where this campaign lives. Steps 6 and 7 belong to the institution, not to
communication — which is the honest limit of what any campaign can do here, and worth
stating plainly to whoever runs it.

## What Makes This Different

For a worker reading nothing else: **name one thing the ecosystem refuses to promise,
explain the mechanism that makes the promises it does keep, and never ask for trust.**

# ═══════════ MARKETING INTELLIGENCE ═══════════

## Communication Objective

To make a claim checkable.

Not to reassure and not to persuade. The objective is that a reader finishes the post
holding a specific, verifiable statement about how the ecosystem behaves — one they
could test, and one that would be embarrassing for the institution if it were false.

## What We Are Really Selling

**Honesty as an operating characteristic.**

The visible content is governance. What is being sold is a prediction about future
behaviour: that this institution will tell you the truth when the truth is
inconvenient — about a bed it does not have, a cost it must charge, a prognosis it
cannot improve.

Restated for a worker: we are not selling quality, safety or excellence. We are selling
**the expectation of candour**, which is the only thing a frightened person actually
needs from an institution and the only thing this campaign can honestly deliver.

## Human Truth

**People forgive institutions that are honest with them, and never forgive ones that
were not.**

An outcome that goes badly is survivable for a relationship. Discovering afterwards
that you were managed rather than informed is not. Every serious reputational failure
in healthcare has this shape, and every audience knows it.

A second truth: **a limit is more informative than a promise.** Anyone can promise;
only someone with a real standard knows precisely where it ends.

## Audience Psychology

How the audience thinks before anything is said:

- **All institutional claims are marketing.** The default, and it is a reasonable
  default.
- **The louder the claim, the weaker the substance.**
- **Nobody admits anything.** So an admission carries disproportionate weight.
- **They'll close ranks if something goes wrong.** The specific fear underneath the
  general distrust, and the one no campaign addresses.
- **Transparency is a word, not a practice.** It has been claimed by everyone and
  demonstrated by almost nobody.

Every one of these is corrected only by behaviour that costs something.

## Emotional Triggers

**Primary:** reassurance through candour.

**Supporting:** respect, relief, confidence, recognition.

Deliberately excluded: fear, indignation, pride, defensiveness, and the register of
moral superiority. This campaign never suggests that others are dishonest — partly
because §21 forbids comparison, and partly because an institution that points at
others' failings invites the audience to look for its own.

## Psychological Barriers

**Barrier 1 — "This is just more marketing."**
The default reading of any trust campaign, and the reason the campaign's own name must
never be spoken. Corrected by leading with a limit: marketing does not surrender
claims.

**Barrier 2 — "Standards on paper mean nothing."**
Held most strongly by physicians, who have seen it. Corrected by describing enforcement
— training, audit, what happens to a finding — rather than the standard itself.

**Barrier 3 — "You only say this when nothing has gone wrong."**
Corrected by publishing how the institution behaves when something does: honest
communication, explanation, alternatives. Never by discussing an actual incident.

**Barrier 4 — "I've been let down before."**
Not addressable by argument. Corrected only by consistency over time, which is why this
campaign must be sustained and must never contradict itself.

**Barrier 5 — "If they were confident they wouldn't need to say it."**
The trap built into the campaign's premise. Corrected by never making trust the
subject: each post is about a mechanism, and trust is the reader's conclusion rather
than the post's topic.

## Core Messaging Framework

Consistently reinforced:

- We will tell you what we cannot promise.
- Our standards are audited, not announced.
- Financial clarity comes at the beginning, not at the end.
- Admission is decided on medical need, never on occupancy.
- The same standard applies at both hospitals, because INSAN sets it.
- We hold our communication to published rules, and this is one of them.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. **The limit** — something the ecosystem will not claim.
2. **The reason** — why that limit exists, in medical or ethical terms.
3. **The mechanism** — what is promised instead, and how it is enforced.
4. **The consistency** — that this holds at both hospitals, and for every audience.
5. **INSAN** — as the layer that sets and audits it.

Never reverse it. Opening with the mechanism produces a governance statement; opening
with the limit produces the surprise that makes it read.

If there is only one line, it is the limit.

## Narrative Themes

- **What we will never promise.** The campaign's strongest single format.
- **The cost conversation at the start.** Financial transparency as a described
  practice.
- **The bed we do not have.** Honest communication about capacity, and the alternative
  guidance that follows.
- **Admission on need.** The ethical rule that occupancy targets never override
  clinical criteria.
- **The standard that gets checked.** Audit, and what happens to a finding.
- **The same answer to everyone.** What is said to an insurer is what is said to a
  patient.
- **The rules we hold our own marketing to.** Publishing §21's red lines is itself a
  trust mechanism.
- **The six parts of trust.** The Trust Equation, one element at a time.
- **When we say we do not know.** Clinical uncertainty, respected rather than
  concealed.
- **Two hospitals, one standard.** Consistency as evidence.

## Content Pillars

**1. What We Will Not Promise** — published limits, with reasons.
**2. How A Standard Is Enforced** — training, audit, consequence.
**3. Money, Said Early** — financial transparency as practice.
**4. Honest When It Is Difficult** — capacity, declined admission, uncertainty.
**5. The Same Everywhere** — consistency across hospitals and across audiences.
**6. The Rules We Set Ourselves** — the ecosystem's published constraints on its own
communication and conduct.

## Storytelling Opportunities

Framings, not topics — each one specific and each one costly to say:

- A published list of sentences the ecosystem will never use in an advertisement.
- The financial conversation happening on day one, before treatment, as a described
  routine.
- A referring physician being told there is no bed, and what is offered instead.
- The clinical criteria for admission, stated plainly, with occupancy explicitly
  excluded.
- An audit finding leading to a protocol change — the mechanism, not the incident.
- The same protocol on a wall at Future and at Delta.
- The six elements of the Trust Equation, one per post, with what each looks like in
  practice.
- A doctor saying "I don't know yet" framed as the standard rather than the exception.
- What a family is told when a treatment is not working.
- The sentence "we cannot guarantee that", written out and explained.

## Communication Style

Always: plain, specific, unhurried, factual, quietly certain.

Never: reassuring in tone, defensive, righteous, warm, or persuasive. Warmth in this
campaign reads as an attempt to influence, which is fatal to it.

The register is closest to a well-written policy explained by a person who believes in
it — flat, exact, and unbothered by whether the reader is impressed.

## Tone of Voice

Write as an institution stating its own rules to itself, in public, and inviting the
reader to hold it to them.

Short declarative sentences. No adjectives of quality. No rhetorical questions. No
appeals.

The most useful discipline: **every sentence should be one the ecosystem could be held
to.** If a sentence cannot be enforced, it is decoration, and decoration in this
campaign is damage.

## Messaging Rules

- Always lead with a limit or a mechanism, never with a claim.
- Always make each statement specific enough to be checkable.
- Always verify with the relevant department before publishing anything descriptive.
- Never use the phrase "trust us", or any variant of it.
- Never make a clinical outcome claim.
- Never publish a figure the operator has not approved.
- Never claim an accreditation, ranking or award not held.
- Never compare, explicitly or implicitly, to another institution.
- Never reference a specific incident, complaint or dispute.
- Never use patient testimony as evidence in this campaign.
- Never adopt a defensive posture, and never answer an accusation nobody made.
- Never let this campaign carry Delta's hospital-level trust story.

# ═══════════ OFFERS ═══════════

## Current Offers

**No offers.** A promotional offer inside a campaign about honesty is
self-invalidating: it reveals a commercial motive at the exact moment the campaign is
asking to be read as non-commercial.

## Offer Rules

- No promotional offer, price, discount or package may appear in this campaign.
- No service may be recommended, even indirectly.
- Financial transparency may be *described as a practice* and must never become a
  claim about being cheaper.

## Call To Action

In priority order, and all of them soft:

1. **None.** Most posts should ask for nothing. An ask converts evidence into a pitch.
2. **Ask us anything** — an open invitation to test the claim, and the only CTA that
   reinforces rather than undermines the campaign.
3. **Read the standard** — where a document or page exists that a reader can actually
   consult.
4. **Get in touch** — for institutional and contract enquiries, on the INSAN page only.

Never a booking CTA, under any circumstances.

## Primary KPI

**Shares and saves, and the sentiment of comments.**

Deliberately not reach or engagement volume. This campaign succeeds when it is
forwarded — the reader sends it to someone who is deciding — and when comments express
recognition rather than argument. A trust post that generates debate has usually
adopted a defensive posture somewhere.

For the commercial audience, the real measure is not a social metric at all: it is
whether the material is usable in a contract conversation. Ask the people having those
conversations.

# ═══════════ STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE ═══════════

## Strategic Reminder

Before publishing, ask what the reader could now hold the ecosystem to.

If the answer is *nothing — it was reassurance*, the post failed. If it is a specific,
inconvenient statement the institution would be embarrassed to be caught contradicting,
it worked.

And ask the second question, which belongs to this campaign alone: **is it true, today,
in both hospitals?** If it is aspirational, it does not run.

## Strategic Constraints

- Never compete by comparison, and never imply that others are less honest.
- Never publish an aspiration as a practice.
- Never respond to criticism through this campaign.
- Never let a marketing objective override an accuracy question.
- Professional ethics take priority over marketing effectiveness
  (`MEDICAL_SERVICE_ICU.md`, Strategic Constraints).
- INSAN's long-term reputation is worth more than any short-term campaign result —
  recorded as permanent, and nowhere more literally true than here.

## Medical Communication Principles

- Every statement must be supportable.
- Clinical uncertainty is respected and may be shown; certainty is never implied where
  medicine cannot ethically provide it.
- The campaign explains the healthcare *process*; it never predicts outcomes.
- Transparency is treated as part of medical ethics rather than as a communication
  technique — which is why the campaign describes it as practice, not as a value.
- Anything describing how a hospital actually behaves must be verified with that
  hospital before it is published.

## Never Promise

The campaign must never promise:

- Any clinical outcome, survival, recovery or prevention.
- Guaranteed admission, bed availability, timing or physician availability.
- That errors, complications or infections cannot occur.
- Any safety record, infection rate, complaint rate or audit score not supplied and
  approved by the operator.
- Accreditation, certification, ranking or award not actually held.
- That the ecosystem is more trustworthy, more transparent or more ethical than any

# ═══════════ INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE ═══════════

## Institutional Knowledge Extensions

**This campaign must never carry Delta's trust story, and the reason is strategic.**
`PROJECT_STRUCTURE.md` assigns Delta an umbrella campaign for rebuilding trust, and
`PROJECT_DECISIONS.md` §4 requires that trust-rebuilding messages be integrated into
general content rather than published as explicit posts about rebuilding trust. A
corporate campaign that took up Delta's local reputation would violate both — it would
make the specific general, and it would say out loud the thing the decision says must
stay implicit.

**A known contradiction that this campaign is exposed to.** Audit B recorded that three
systems describing the ecosystem's medical centers agree on 18% of them, and that the
public website advertises centers — Ophthalmology and Dermatology — that no campaign
supports. A campaign whose entire premise is consistency is directly undermined by a
public website that contradicts the campaign system. **This should be resolved before
this campaign is scheduled**, and whoever runs it should check it first. It is a
brand-owner decision, not a content problem.

**Why patient testimony is excluded, recorded so it is not re-litigated.** Testimony is
the standard instrument of trust marketing and it is the wrong instrument here.
Testimony evidences one experience; the ecosystem's claim is that the experience does
not vary. A satisfied patient proves that a good outcome is possible, which nobody
doubted. The claim under examination is systemic, so the evidence must be systemic.
Testimony has its own campaigns — *Success Stories* and *Patient Journey* — and works
properly there.

**Costly honesty has an internal cost too.** Publishing a limit means the institution
can be held to it, including by its own staff and by patients quoting it back. That is
the mechanism working as intended, and whoever runs this campaign should expect it
rather than be surprised by it. A published limit that the organisation then breaches
is worse than never having published it — which is why the verification rule in *Core
Principles* is not procedural caution but the campaign's central risk control.

**What this file cannot supply.** Any actual audit result, infection rate, complaint
statistic, accreditation status, incident record or governance document. It describes
mechanisms recorded in the committed documents; it certifies nothing about current
practice. Every descriptive statement must be verified with the hospital before
publication.
